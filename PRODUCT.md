# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small landlords in Ontario with 1–5 properties. They bought a rental to build quiet wealth, not to manage a second job. They're self-managing because traditional property managers cost 10–15% (money they don't have) and enterprise PM software like Buildium or AppFolio was built for operators with 50+ units and full-time staff. They feel underserved, overwhelmed, and like no one built anything for them specifically.

They know their pain intimately: chasing rent on the 3rd, Googling N4 forms at 11pm, fielding 2am maintenance texts, staring at spreadsheets, watching their equity evaporate in management fees. They've tried to make it work. They're tired.

## Product Purpose

Prospera Platform is a mobile-first property management app built specifically for the Ontario landlord with 1–5 properties. It automates rent collection, files N4s automatically when rent is late, triages maintenance requests with AI, and handles lease renewals with real market data. It's not a lighter version of enterprise software — it's a different category entirely.

This is a waitlist landing page. The goal is to get landlords to join the waitlist with their email. The conviction that should carry them there: "this was built by someone who has lived my exact problem, and it will give me my life back."

## Brand Personality

Empathetic, direct, inevitable. Not startup-hype. Not corporate polish. The voice of someone who has been in the room — who knows the exact moment rent was 2 days late and you didn't know what to say. Credibility through specificity. Confidence without arrogance.

The founder (Ebin Jaison) is young, has hands-on experience, and built this because nothing existed for him. That story is the brand.

## Anti-references

- Buildium, AppFolio, Propertyware — enterprise PM software. Clinical, feature-list-driven, built for their team not yours.
- Generic "SaaS cream and purple" landing pages. Helvetica headlines, feature grids, pricing tables. Invisible.
- Traditional property management company websites. Conservative, stock-photo, "trust us" energy.
- Zillow, Realtor.ca — marketplace energy. Not a tool built for the landlord.
- Any design that looks like "AI made that." If a visitor can guess the tool from the aesthetic, it failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Speak the pain before the solution.** The landlord has to feel seen before they'll believe anything. Copy leads with their exact Tuesday, not product benefits.
2. **Specificity is the credibility.** "Your mortgage renewed. $1,800 became $3,200. You're collecting $2,800." That sentence beats any feature list. Use the exact number, the exact time, the exact feeling.
3. **The founder's story is the proof.** Not testimonials, not case studies — the fact that someone built this because they needed it and couldn't find it. That's the trust signal.
4. **New category, not better app.** Every design decision should feel like a different way of being a landlord, not a cheaper Buildium. The framing is liberation, not efficiency.
5. **Earn the scroll.** Each section has one job. Don't pad, don't repeat, don't hedge. If a section doesn't earn its place on the page, cut it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. The audience skews 30–55 and is not necessarily tech-native. Clear contrast ratios on all text. No motion that can't be reduced. Large tap targets on mobile.
