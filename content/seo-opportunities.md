## New Topic Opportunities
Topics found in competitor/search results that we have no post covering. Blog agent should prioritize these above the default keyword list.

- **N1 Form Ontario: Step-by-Step Guide to Serving a Rent Increase Notice**: Standalone competitor posts (tenon10.com, foundspaces.ca, nihanglaw.ca) rank specifically for "N1 form Ontario 2026." Our `rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md` covers the guideline percentage but not the mechanics of completing and serving the form. Angle: how to calculate the correct new rent using the 2.1% guideline, the mandatory 90-day notice rule, how and where to serve Form N1, what voids the increase (wrong form, late delivery, incorrect calculation), and how to re-serve if you make an error.

- **L2 Application Ontario: Eviction for Causes Beyond Non-Payment**: We have `l1-application-ltb-ontario.md` and `l9-application-ontario.md` but no post on the L2 — the application landlords file after serving N5, N8, or N9 notices. Confirmed gap across multiple competitor posts (georgebrown.biz, mardamanagement.com, olhgroup.ca). Angle: when you need an L2 vs. L1, which N-form must come first, filing fees, what evidence to bring, how the hearing differs from a non-payment hearing, and typical LTB outcomes.

- **LTB Mediation at the Landlord and Tenant Board: Should You Settle?**: We cover hearing prep (`ltb-hearing-preparation-ontario-landlords.md`) but not the mediation process itself. Multiple competitor posts (mtsparalegalservices.com, civilparalegal.com, landlordselfhelp.com) rank for "LTB mediation Ontario." Angle: what dispute resolution officers do, how to request mediation on your hearing date, consent order vs. adjudicated order, when settling is strategically smarter than a full hearing, what to negotiate (payment plan structure, move-out date, rent abatement limits), and what happens if the tenant breaks a consent order.

- **Fixed-Term Lease Rules in Ontario After Bill 60 (2026)**: We have `bill-60-ontario-landlords.md` which covers this broadly, but a dedicated post targeting "fixed-term lease Ontario 2026" captures landlords searching that specific question. Competitors (tenon10.com, nihanglaw.ca) have standalone guides. Angle: old automatic month-to-month conversion vs. new rules, how to serve notice of non-renewal, what tenants can do in response, and how to use fixed-term leases strategically for shorter-commitment rentals.

- **LTB Hearing Wait Times Ontario 2026: Realistic Timelines by Application Type**: Standalone top-ranking search intent ("how long does LTB take," "LTB backlog 2026"). We have `how-long-does-eviction-take-ontario.md` but not a breakdown by application type. Current reality: L1 (non-payment) averages 4–6 months; L2/N12 (personal use, other grounds) averages 6–9 months. Angle: wait times by application type with sources, what landlords can do during the wait (L9 for arrears, repayment agreements, interim orders), how Bill 60 aimed to address backlogs, and realistic cash flow planning for the waiting period.

- **N6 Notice Ontario: Evicting a Tenant for Illegal Acts**: We cover N4, N5, N8, N12, N13 — the N6 (Notice to Terminate a Tenancy Early for Illegal Acts or Misrepresentation) is a clear gap that appears in every "complete LTB forms" competitor post (mardamanagement.com, olhgroup.ca, foundspaces.ca). Angle: what qualifies as an illegal act (drug activity, weapons, human trafficking), the 10-day termination period, how it differs from N5, evidence requirements (police reports, documentation), what the LTB looks for, and the N6 vs. emergency hearing path.

- **Ontario Human Rights Code for Landlords: Lawful Tenant Selection**: Appears in tenant screening and credit check searches — competitors rank for "OHRC landlord tenant selection" and "source of income protected ground Ontario." Distinct from `tenant-credit-check-ontario-landlords.md` and `tenant-screening-red-flags.md`. Angle: the 16 protected grounds, what questions landlords cannot ask, why rejecting ODSP/OW applicants as income source is illegal, how to document selection decisions to be defensible at an HRTO hearing.

- **Requiring Tenant Insurance in Ontario: What Landlords Can and Cannot Do**: Multiple competitor articles rank for this (marathoninsurance.ca, mardamanagement.com, tenantrights.ca). We have `rental-property-insurance-ontario.md` (landlord's own policy) but nothing about requiring tenants to carry their own. Angle: whether it's legal to require it (yes, if written into the lease), liability-only vs. contents coverage, how to draft the lease clause, how to request proof, and what happens if a tenant refuses — including LTB enforcement options.

- **London Ontario Rental Market 2026: What Falling Rents Mean for Landlords**: The Harrison Carter Group ranks for this directly ("Are Rental Prices Falling in London Ontario? 2026"). Rising vacancy rates and increased rental supply are pushing average rents down in London. No existing post addresses current market conditions. Angle: what the data shows for London/St. Thomas/Strathroy, pricing strategy when competing for fewer tenants, retention tactics to avoid turnover costs, and how professional property management adds value in a tenant's market.

- **Complete LTB Forms Directory for Ontario Landlords**: Multiple competitors have a master "all LTB forms explained" index (foundspaces.ca, mardamanagement.com, olhgroup.ca) that serves as a high-traffic hub and earns backlinks. We have individual N-form posts but no overview index. Angle: every N-form (N1–N13) with one-line description and link to our dedicated post; every L-form (L1, L2, L9, L10) with use-case, filing fee, portal filing steps, and current processing timelines.

## Topics to Expand

Add a concise N1 vs. N2 section distinguishing rent-controlled from exempt units. Competitors are outranking us with standalone N1 form guides that target this same search intent.

- **landlord-tenant-board-ontario-guide.md** — Add a 2026 wait times section (L1: 4–6 months, L2/N12: 6–9 months) and a practical walkthrough of the Tribunals Ontario Portal: how to create an account, upload evidence at least 7 days before the hearing, and check file status. Also add a mediation section — many landlords don't know they can request mediation on the day of their hearing.

- **bill-60-ontario-landlords.md** — Add a dedicated section on fixed-term lease changes with examples of how landlords can use them strategically. The fixed-term change is Bill 60's most significant practical impact for day-to-day landlords and should be given more prominence.
