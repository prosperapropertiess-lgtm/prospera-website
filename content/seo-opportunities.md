## New Topic Opportunities
Topics found in competitor/search results that we have no post covering. Blog agent should prioritize these above the default keyword list.

- **Bill 60 Ontario: What Every Landlord Needs to Know (2026)**: Bill 60 (Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, passed November 2025) appears across virtually every search query and is the single biggest legislative change in Ontario landlord-tenant law in years. Key changes: N4 notice period cut from 14 to 7 days; fixed-term leases no longer auto-convert to month-to-month; N12 compensation waived if landlord gives 120+ days notice; appeal window shortened from 30 to 15 days; tenants must pay 50% of arrears before raising maintenance defenses at eviction hearings. We have `ontario-landlord-tenant-act-2026.md` but no post specifically targeting "Bill 60 Ontario landlord" as a search phrase — this is the term searchers use and it should have its own post.

- **N12 Notice Ontario: Complete Guide to Personal-Use Evictions**: We cover N4 and N5 in dedicated posts but have no post for the N12 (Notice to End Tenancy for Landlord's Own Use). This is heavily searched and has significant Bill 60 nuance: 60-day notice requires one month's compensation, but 120-day notice now exempts the landlord from paying. Good-faith requirement, T5 bad-faith penalty (up to 12 months rent), and new LTB disclosure rules (landlords must report all N12s issued in past 2 years) make this a rich topic with high user intent.

- **N8 Notice Ontario: Ending Tenancy for Persistent Late Payment**: We have N4 (non-payment) and N5 (interference/damage) but not N8 (end of tenancy at end of term for habitual late payers or persistent behaviour). Competitors rank for "N8 Ontario landlord" and the form appears in N4/N8/N12 combo searches. Angle: when to use N8 instead of repeated N4s, evidence requirements (6-12 months of payment ledger), timing rules, and how it pairs with an L2 application.

- **How to Prepare for Your LTB Hearing: A Landlord's Evidence Guide**: Multiple competitor pages rank for "LTB hearing preparation," "disclosure for LTB hearings," and "self-represented landlords at LTB." We have a general LTB guide but nothing on the practical mechanics of winning your hearing — organizing exhibits A-E, submitting evidence 7 days before the date, conducting yourself in a Teams videoconference hearing, what adjudicators look for. High search volume and directly relevant to landlords mid-process.

- **Ontario Standard Lease 2026: What Every Landlord Must Include**: "Ontario Standard Lease 2026: Complete Guide for Landlords" is a top-ranking competitor post and a frequent search. We have `how-to-write-lease-agreement-ontario.md` but not a post focused on the mandatory government form (Form 2229E), its 17 required sections, which optional clauses are enforceable, what happens if you fail to provide it (tenant can withhold one month's rent and give 60-day notice). Angle: walk through the standard form section-by-section for landlords new to the required form.

- **How to Legally Run a Credit Check on Tenants in Ontario**: We have `tenant-screening-red-flags.md` but no post on the mechanics and legal limits of credit checking. This is a distinct search intent ("how to run credit check Ontario landlord"). Key angles: written consent required, SIN cannot be demanded, thin-file applicants cannot be rejected solely for lack of credit history (HRC obligation), what credit bureaus (Equifax/TransUnion) to use, PIPEDA data-retention rules. Competitors rank well for this and it directly supports our tenant screening coverage.

- **N1 Form Ontario: Step-by-Step Guide to Serving a Rent Increase Notice**: "Ontario Rent Increases 2026: The Complete N1 Guide" is a standalone top-ranking competitor post. Our `rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md` covers the guideline percentage but likely doesn't walk through Form N1 mechanics: how to calculate the new amount, the 90-day rule, how N1 (rent-controlled units) differs from N2 (exempt units first occupied after Nov 15, 2018), what voids the notice, and how to file if the tenant disputes it. This targets a different search intent than our existing post.

- **LTB Hearing Wait Times in Ontario: What Landlords Should Expect in 2026**: Standalone high-traffic search ("how long does LTB take," "LTB backlog Ontario 2026"). Current reality: L1 (non-payment) hearings average 4–6 months; L2 personal-use (N12) hearings average 6–9 months; tenant T6 maintenance applications average 8–12 months. This sets expectations for landlords and naturally cross-links to our N4, LTB guide, and eviction posts. Competitors rank dedicated pages for this topic.

## Topics to Expand
Existing posts that are likely thin on content or contain outdated information and could be expanded:

- **n4-notice-ontario.md** — Bill 60 reduced the N4 notice period from 14 days to 7 days for non-payment of rent. This is a significant change; the post must be updated to reflect the new timeline or it will conflict with current law and damage credibility.

- **evicting-tenant-ontario.md** — Needs Bill 60 updates: 7-day N4 period, fixed-term leases no longer auto-converting to month-to-month, N12 compensation exemption at 120-day notice, appeal window shortened to 15 days, and the new 50%-arrears-paid rule for tenants raising maintenance defenses. This is likely the most outdated post on the site given the scope of Bill 60 changes.

- **rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md** — Should state the 2026 guideline explicitly (2.1%, down from 2.5% in 2025), add a step-by-step N1 form section, and clarify the N1 vs N2 distinction (rent-controlled vs exempt units). Competitors are outranking us with dedicated N1 guides.

- **landlord-tenant-board-ontario-guide.md** — Expand with current 2026 wait times by application type (L1: 4–6 months, L2/N12: 6–9 months, T6: 8–12 months) and a practical section on how to submit evidence via the LTB e-File Portal before the 7-day deadline.
