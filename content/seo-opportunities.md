## New Topic Opportunities
Topics found in competitor/search results that we have no post covering. Blog agent should prioritize these above the default keyword list.

- **L1 Application: How to File with the LTB After Serving an N4**: Our `n4-notice-ontario.md` ends at "serve the notice" — but landlords searching "how to file L1 Ontario" and "L1 application LTB" land on competitor sites (Marda Management, George Brown PC, Landlord's Self Help Centre). This is the natural next step in the non-payment flow and a high-intent query. Angle: when you can file (day after termination date on the N4), Tribunals Ontario Portal step-by-step, required documents (copy of N4, Certificate of Service), L1/L9 update form before the hearing, filing fees, and what mistakes void the application.

- **Enforcing an Eviction Order in Ontario: Working with the Sheriff**: We cover the LTB hearing but have nothing on what happens *after* getting the order. The Court Enforcement Office (Sheriff) is the only body that can physically remove a tenant — landlords who act themselves (change locks, remove belongings) commit an illegal lockout. High competitor coverage from Green Economy Law, openroom.ca, Landlord's Self Help Centre. Angle: filing with the Court Enforcement Office, required documents and fees, Sheriff scheduling timeline, 72-hour rule for tenant belongings, and common landlord mistakes after an order is issued.

- **Rent Repayment Agreements: What Ontario Landlords Need to Know**: Appears prominently in non-payment and arrears search results. The LTB expects evidence that a landlord attempted a repayment plan before granting an eviction order — landlords who skip this step often have orders refused or delayed. Distinct from `late-rent-payments-ontario.md` (which covers the N4 process) — this is the negotiation and documentation angle. Angle: how to draft a written repayment agreement, what to include (amounts, dates, breach clause), the LTB's formal Payment Agreement form, filing it before the hearing, and what happens if the tenant defaults on the plan.

- **Legal Basement Apartment Requirements in Ontario (2026)**: Very high search volume with multiple competitor sites publishing dedicated 2026 guides (nrbuilds.ca, bedrockbasements.ca, buildersontario.com). Landlords adding a basement unit need: building permit, OBC minimum ceiling height (1.95 m), egress window requirements, ESA electrical certificate, separate entrance, and zoning bylaw clearance. Fines for illegal suites reach $25,000. Local angle: London/Middlesex and Elgin County permit processes, timelines, and typical costs. Strong lead-generation angle — these landlords often need property management once the unit is ready.

- **Ontario Human Rights Code for Landlords: Tenant Selection Compliance**: Appears in every tenant screening and credit check search. The OHRC governs what landlords can and cannot use in selection decisions — receipt of public assistance (ODSP, OW) is a protected ground, meaning landlords cannot reject an applicant because their income comes from government benefits. High complaint and fine risk. Distinct from `tenant-credit-check-ontario-landlords.md`. Angle: the 16 protected grounds, what questions to avoid on applications, income *verification* vs. income *source*, how to document selection decisions, and what to do when an applicant files an HRTO complaint.

- **Ontario Rent Control Exemptions: Is Your Unit Exempt from the 2.1% Cap?**: Many landlords don't know whether their unit is subject to rent control. Units first occupied after November 15, 2018 can set rent freely between tenancies — the 2026 guideline (2.1%) does not apply. Landlords of newer builds are using the N2 form (not N1) for rent increases and can price vacancies at market rate. No existing post covers the exemption specifically. Angle: how to determine if your unit qualifies, what "first occupied" means, N2 form vs N1, vacant-unit pricing rights, and how to correctly notify a sitting tenant in an exempt unit.

- **N1 Form Ontario: Step-by-Step Guide to Serving a Rent Increase Notice**: "Ontario Rent Increases 2026: The Complete N1 Guide" is a standalone top-ranking competitor post (tenon10.com, foundspaces.ca). Our `rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md` covers the guideline percentage but not the mechanics of Form N1. Angle: how to calculate the correct new rent amount, the 90-day written notice rule, N1 (rent-controlled) vs N2 (exempt), how to serve the form, what voids the increase, and how to re-serve if you make an error.

- **LTB Hearing Wait Times in Ontario 2026: What Landlords Should Realistically Expect**: Standalone high-traffic search ("how long does LTB take," "LTB backlog Ontario 2026"). Carried forward from the previous cycle — still not covered. Current reality: L1 (non-payment) hearings average 4–6 months; L2/N12 (personal use) average 6–9 months. Sets expectations and naturally cross-links to our N4, LTB guide, and eviction posts. Angle: wait times by application type, what landlords can do to manage cash flow during the wait (repayment agreements, interim orders), and how Bill 60 aimed to reduce backlogs.

## Topics to Expand
Existing posts that are likely thin on content or contain outdated information:

- **n4-notice-ontario.md** — Bill 60 reduced the N4 termination period from 14 days to 7 days for most tenancies. Verify the post reflects this; if not, it conflicts with current law. Also add an explicit "What Happens Next" section that walks from the N4 to the L1 application — landlords read the N4 post and then don't know the next step.

- **evicting-tenant-ontario.md** — Needs enforcement coverage: what happens after the LTB issues the order (Sheriff, Court Enforcement Office, 72-hour tenant belongings rule). Currently the post likely ends at "the LTB grants your order" — the actual removal step is absent, which is the part landlords most want to understand.

- **rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md** — State the 2026 guideline explicitly (2.1%), add a concise N1 vs N2 section for rent-controlled vs. exempt units, and include a step-by-step checklist for completing and serving Form N1. Competitors are outranking us with standalone N1 guides targeting this same intent.

- **landlord-tenant-board-ontario-guide.md** — Add a section on current 2026 wait times by application type and a practical walkthrough of the Tribunals Ontario Portal (how to create an account, upload evidence 7 days before the hearing date, check file status).
