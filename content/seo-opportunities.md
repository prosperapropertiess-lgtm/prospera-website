## New Topic Opportunities
Topics found in competitor/search results that we have no post covering. Blog agent should prioritize these above the default keyword list.

- **L9 Application Ontario: Collect Rent Without Evicting Your Tenant**: High-intent gap confirmed by multiple competitor guides (georgebrown.biz, mardamanagement.com, badtenantcleanups.com, tribunalsontario.ca). We have `l1-application-ltb-ontario.md` but the L9 is a completely different tool — it collects arrears while keeping the tenancy intact. Ideal for landlords who have a good tenant who fell behind. Angle: when to choose L9 vs L1, the N4 prerequisite, what the LTB order looks like, how to enforce via Small Claims Court if unpaid, and when L9 becomes the wrong tool (tenant leaves, arrears grow).

- **Legal Basement Apartment Requirements Ontario (2026)**: 10+ standalone competitor articles ranking for this keyword (buildersontario.com, nrbuilds.ca, bedrockbasements.ca, aduontario.com). London landlords frequently add basement units — Middlesex and Elgin County permit requirements apply. Carried forward from last cycle — still not published. Angle: OBC minimum ceiling height (1.95 m), egress window specs, fire separation rating (45-min), ESA electrical certificate, separate entrance requirement, zoning bylaw step, 2026 Building Code updates (radon protection, insulation), permit costs and timelines, and $25,000 fine risk for illegal suites.

- **Ontario Rent Control Exemptions: Is Your Unit Exempt from the 2.1% Guideline?**: Units first occupied after November 15, 2018 are not subject to the guideline — landlords can price vacancies at market rate and raise rent by any amount between tenancies. No existing post covers the exemption directly. High search demand confirmed ("rent increase Ontario 2026," "N2 form Ontario"). Angle: how to determine if your unit qualifies, what "first occupied" means on a new build, N2 form vs N1, how to price a vacancy in an exempt unit, and how to correctly serve notice to a sitting tenant in an exempt building.

- **N1 Form Ontario: Step-by-Step Guide to Serving a Rent Increase Notice**: Standalone competitor posts (tenon10.com, foundspaces.ca, nihanglaw.ca) rank specifically for "N1 form Ontario 2026." Our `rent-increase-ontario-guidelines.md` covers the guideline percentage but not the mechanics of completing and serving the form. Angle: how to calculate the correct new rent amount using the 2.1% guideline, the mandatory 90-day notice rule, how and where to serve Form N1, what voids the increase (wrong form, late delivery, incorrect calculation), and how to re-serve if you make an error.

- **Ontario Human Rights Code for Landlords: Lawful Tenant Selection**: Appears in tenant screening and credit check search results — competitors rank for "OHRC landlord tenant selection," "source of income protected ground Ontario." The OHRC prohibits rejecting applicants because income comes from ODSP or OW. Distinct from `tenant-credit-check-ontario-landlords.md` and `tenant-screening-red-flags.md`. Angle: the 16 protected grounds, what questions landlords cannot ask, income verification vs income source, documenting selection decisions, and responding if an applicant files an HRTO complaint.

- **N6 Notice Ontario: Evicting a Tenant for Illegal Acts**: We cover N4, N5, N8, N12, N13 — the N6 (Notice to Terminate a Tenancy Early for Illegal Acts or Misrepresentation) is a clear gap that appears in every "complete LTB forms" competitor post. Angle: what qualifies as an illegal act (drug activity, weapons, human trafficking), the 10-day termination period, how it differs from N5, evidence requirements (police reports, documentation), what the LTB looks for, and the N6 vs. emergency hearing path.

- **Requiring Tenant Insurance in Ontario: What Landlords Can and Cannot Do**: Multiple competitor articles rank for this (marathoninsurance.ca, mardamanagement.com, tenantrights.ca). We have `rental-property-insurance-ontario.md` (landlord's own policy) but nothing about requiring tenants to carry their own. Distinct topic with clear search demand. Angle: whether it's legal to require it (yes, if written into the lease), liability-only vs. contents coverage, how to draft the lease clause, how to request proof, and what happens if a tenant refuses — including LTB enforcement options.

- **Complete LTB Forms Directory for Ontario Landlords**: Multiple competitors have a master "all LTB forms explained" index (foundspaces.ca, mardamanagement.com, azilaw.com, topromanage.com) that serves as a high-traffic hub and earns backlinks. We have individual N-form posts but no overview index. Angle: every N-form (N1–N13) with one-line description and link to our dedicated post where it exists; every L-form (L1, L2, L9, L10) with use-case, filing fee ($186 online / $201 in-person), portal filing steps, and current processing timelines by application type.

- **Fixed-Term Lease Rules in Ontario After Bill 60 (2026)**: Bill 60's most landlord-friendly change — fixed-term leases no longer automatically convert to month-to-month at expiry, giving landlords the option to end tenancies or renegotiate terms. We have `bill-60-ontario-landlords.md` which covers this broadly, but a dedicated post targeting "fixed-term lease Ontario 2026" captures landlords searching that specific question. Angle: old rules vs. new rules, when a fixed-term ends without conversion, how to serve notice of non-renewal, what tenants can do in response, and how to use fixed-term leases strategically for shorter-commitment rentals.

- **LTB Hearing Wait Times Ontario 2026: Realistic Timelines by Application Type**: Standalone top-ranking search intent ("how long does LTB take," "LTB backlog 2026"). Carried forward — still not published. Current reality: L1 (non-payment) averages 4–6 months; L2/N12 (personal use/other grounds) averages 6–9 months. Angle: wait times by application type with sources, what landlords can do during the wait (L9 for arrears, repayment agreements, interim orders), how Bill 60 aimed to address backlogs, and realistic cash flow planning for the waiting period.
