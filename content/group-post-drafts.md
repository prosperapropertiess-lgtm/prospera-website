## 2026-05-04
Anyone else dealt with an N4 getting thrown out at the LTB?

The three things that seem to trip up landlords most often:
- Including NSF fees or utilities in the rent amount (they don't belong on the N4)
- Wrong termination date — 14 days minimum, must fall on or after the end of the rental period
- Serving it the same day rent is due instead of the day after

Get one of these wrong and your whole L1 is back to square one.

What's the worst N4 mistake you've seen — or had happen to you? Asking because we see this constantly in London and it costs landlords months of time.

## 2026-05-05
Anyone here ever filed an L5 for an above-guideline rent increase?

Most landlords I talk to have never filed one — even after putting a new roof on, replacing the furnace, or dealing with a big spike in operating costs. The annual rent guideline doesn't cover major capital expenditures — that's literally what the AGI process is for.

Things that trip people up: vague invoices, missing contractor info, trying to claim cosmetic work that doesn't qualify. The LTB scrutinizes these applications closely.

The math can actually be meaningful — a $50,000 capital project spread across several units adds up to a real monthly increase that's fully LTB-sanctioned.

Who's successfully gotten one approved, and what was the hardest part of the process?
