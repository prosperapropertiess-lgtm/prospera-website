## Ontario Property Management Fee Breakdown: What You'll Actually Pay

### Monthly Management Fee (Your Base Cost)

This is the percentage of rent you pay every month, regardless of what happens.

**Ontario market range: 8–15% of monthly rent collected**

Real dollar examples for a $1,900/month residential unit:
- **8% = $152/month** ($1,824/year) — typically independent operators or high-volume portfolios
- **10% = $190/month** ($2,280/year) — mid-market standard
- **12% = $228/month** ($2,736/year) — full-service companies
- **15% = $285/month** ($3,420/year) — boutique or premium services

**The annual difference between 8% and 15%: $1,596 for identical service.**

**What must be included in the base fee:**
- Monthly rent collection and bank deposits
- Tenant communication (phone, email, online portal)
- Maintenance request coordination and follow-up
- Monthly financial statements
- Annual property inspections
- Basic lease enforcement
- Tenant complaints and conflict resolution

**Red flag #1:** If a company lists basic tenant communication, portal access, or monthly statements as *separate* line items, walk away. These are baseline services.

### Tenant Placement Fee (The Commission Nobody Talks About)

This is charged once per new tenant and is where property management companies extract the most profit—sometimes at the cost of your interests.

**Ontario range: 50–100% of one month's rent per placement**

For a $1,900/month unit: **$950–$1,900 per tenant placed**

**Three critical questions before signing:**

1. **Is there a guarantee or re-placement period?** (If tenant breaks lease within 60–90 days, do you place again for free?)
2. **Is this negotiable for longer leases or multi-property contracts?**
3. **Does this fee apply to lease renewals with the same tenant?** (It shouldn't—see next section.)

**Why this matters:** A company earning $1,900 per tenant placement has financial incentive to tolerate higher turnover. Ask any prospective manager about their average tenant retention rate. A good company should hold tenants 3+ years.

**Red flag #2:** High placement fees paired with weak guarantees or re-placement clauses.

### Lease Renewal Fee (Pure Profit on Existing Tenants)

Some Ontario property managers charge $150–$500 to renew a lease with a tenant who's already screened, approved, and paying on time. This is where incentive misalignment becomes clearest.

**The reality:** Renewing an existing lease takes 10% of the effort of placing a new tenant. Charging 50% of the placement fee (or sometimes the full amount) is overreach.

**What to demand:** Zero lease renewal fee, or a flat $50–$100 administrative charge maximum. Keeping a quality tenant is the entire goal of long-term property management. If your manager is charging to renew, they don't think like an owner.

**Red flag #3:** Lease renewal fee equal to or higher than 25% of placement fee.

### Maintenance Coordination & Contractor Markups (The Invisible Tax)

This is where the biggest conflicts of interest hide.

**Common Ontario practice: 10–20% markup on all contractor invoices**

Real example: Furnace repair costs $800 from the contractor. You're billed $800 + 15% = **$920**.

Over one year, if you spend $5,000 on maintenance and repairs, you're paying **$500–$1,000 in invisible fees**. This creates perverse incentive—the more expensive repairs are approved, the more the manager earns.

**The problem:** Your manager may approve unnecessary work, fail to get competitive quotes, or recommend expensive contractors over budget-friendly ones.

**What to demand in writing:**
- Cost-only pass-through on all maintenance (no markup), OR
- Fixed coordination fee per repair (flat $25–$50 per work order, not percentage-based)
- Contractor quotes must be shared with you before approval for repairs over $300
- Right to approve contractor selection

This is non-negotiable. [Learn exactly what maintenance responsibilities landlords have in Ontario](/blog/landlord-maintenance-responsibilities-ontario) to understand which repairs you're actually obligated to fund.

**Red flag #4:** Manager refuses to disclose markup percentage or insists it's "standard."

### Vacancy Fees (Charging You During Loss)

You've already lost 3 months of rent from a vacancy. Some property managers then charge you an additional fee during that period.

**Ontario practice varies:**
- **Best:** No vacancy fee (you pay normal percentage only on rent collected)
- **Acceptable:** Reduced fee during vacancy (50% of normal monthly fee)
- **Avoid:** Full monthly fee charged during vacancy, or additional "vacancy administration" fee

A vacancy already costs roughly 25% of your annual income. Adding fees on top compounds the loss.

**Red flag #5:** Full monthly fee charged during months with zero rent collected, or a separate "vacancy fee" on top.

## How to Compare Property Managers: 8 Questions That Reveal Everything

Stop comparing just the monthly percentage. Calculate the **total annual cost** for your specific property, including all fees. Ask these eight questions in order—the answers reveal how transparent and owner-focused the company is:

1. **"What's your monthly management fee as a percentage, and what's included in that fee?"** (Get the full written list of services.)

2. **"What's your tenant placement fee and what's your re-placement guarantee?"** (If they break lease in 60 days, do you place again free?)

3. **"Do you charge to renew leases with existing tenants?"** (The only acceptable answer is "no" or "$50 flat fee maximum.")

4. **"Do you mark up contractor invoices, and if so, by what percentage?"** (Demand cost-only or flat fee per work order.)

5. **"What do you charge during vacancy, and how is it calculated?"** (Should be zero or reduced fee on rent collected, not a separate fee.)

6. **"What's the term of the agreement, and what are the exit terms if I want to leave?"** (Avoid contracts longer than 2 years or with heavy penalties.)

7. **"Are there any other fees I should know about—setup, inspection, photography, portal, technology, or anything else?"** (Listen for hesitation or vague answers. That's a red flag.)

8. **"Can you provide three references from landlords with similar properties who've been with you 2+ years?"** (Call them. Ask if they've experienced surprise fees.)

If any answer is vague, contradicted by the written contract, or the company seems defensive, move to the next option. Transparency is how you identify professional operators.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a reasonable property management fee in Ontario?**

A: The Ontario market standard is 8–15% of monthly rent for residential properties. For a $1,900/month unit, that's $150–$285/month. The right fee depends on property type, portfolio size, and what's included. Always request a complete written breakdown of base fee, placement fee, renewal fees, maintenance markups, and any other charges. The cheapest option often masks hidden fees that cost more over 12 months.

**Q: Do I have to pay a tenant placement fee every time?**

A: Most Ontario property managers charge 50–100% of one month's rent per new tenant placement. Always ask about a guarantee or re-placement period—reputable companies will place a replacement tenant at no extra cost if the first one breaks lease within 60–90 days. Also ask whether placement fees apply to lease renewals (they shouldn't). Fees are negotiable, especially if you own multiple properties or sign longer agreements.

**Q: Why do some property managers charge a maintenance markup?**

A: A 10–20% markup on contractor invoices is common but creates a dangerous conflict of interest. The more expensive repairs are, the more the manager earns. This incentivizes approving unnecessary work, failing to shop for competitive quotes, or recommending expensive contractors. Demand cost-only pass-through or a fixed coordination fee instead. [Understand what maintenance you're legally responsible for](/blog/landlord-maintenance-responsibilities-ontario) to avoid being pushed into unnecessary spending.

**Q: Can I negotiate property management fees in Ontario?**

A: Yes. Nearly all fees are negotiable, especially if you own multiple properties, have strong tenant profiles, or sign longer agreements. Negotiate the monthly percentage, placement fee, lease renewal fee, maintenance markup, vacancy treatment, setup costs, and contract term. Get all negotiated terms in writing before signing. Don't accept "standard fees"—they rarely are.

**Q: Should I hire a property manager if I only have one rental property?**

A: For most single-property landlords, professional management is worth the cost. One missed [eviction notice](/blog/evicting-tenant-ontario), one bad tenant screening, or one [major maintenance failure](/blog/landlord-maintenance-responsibilities-ontario) can cost thousands more than a year of management fees. [Learn when hiring a property manager makes financial sense](/blog/when-to-hire-a-property-manager-ontario) and [how to find the right fit](/blog/how-to-find-a-property-manager-ontario). Risk mitigation alone justifies the investment.

**Q: What should I do if my current property manager's fees are unclear?**

A: Request a written fee schedule itemizing every charge—monthly fee, placement fee, markups, renewal fees, vacancy treatment, and any other costs. If they won't provide one in writing, or if the contract language is deliberately vague, that's a sign to [switch to a different management company](/blog/switching-property-management-companies-ontario). Transparency is non-negotiable in professional property management.
