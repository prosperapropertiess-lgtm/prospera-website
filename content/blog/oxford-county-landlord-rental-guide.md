Oxford County sits between London and the Greater Toronto Area, covering the City of Woodstock, the Town of Ingersoll, the Town of Tillsonburg, and several smaller municipalities including Norwich, Zorra, and East Zorra-Tavistock. If you rent residential property anywhere in Oxford County, the rules work differently from London in one key way: there is no county-wide or municipal rental housing licensing program.

That is the good news. The other news is that everything else still applies — the Residential Tenancies Act, the Ontario Building Code, the Fire Code, and each municipality's own property standards bylaw. This guide explains what Oxford County landlords need to know in Woodstock, Ingersoll, and Tillsonburg, and where landlords in these communities typically make mistakes.

---

## No Rental Housing Licence in Oxford County

London requires landlords to hold a Rental Housing Licence and renew it annually. Oxford County municipalities — including Woodstock, Ingersoll, and Tillsonburg — have no equivalent program as of 2026.

That means:

- No annual licensing fee
- No licence-triggered mandatory inspection cycle
- No licence renewal window that tracks your compliance history
- No licence requirement before you can list a unit

What you do have is property standards enforcement. Each municipality maintains a by-law that sets minimum conditions for all residential properties. If a tenant, a neighbour, or a bylaw officer files a complaint, a Property Standards Officer can inspect your rental unit regardless of whether you hold a licence. You are not invisible to enforcement — there is simply no proactive licensing regime.

The practical implication: you won't get a renewal notice forcing you to book an inspection every year. That makes it easier to let maintenance drift. It also makes it easier to miss the provincial compliance obligations that do apply — rent increases, deposit interest, entry rights, and lease documentation — without the annual check-in that London's licensing system creates.

## The RTA Applies Everywhere in Ontario

The Residential Tenancies Act covers every residential rental unit in Ontario. The rules for rent increases, entry notice, deposits, lease agreements, and Landlord and Tenant Board proceedings are identical in Woodstock, Ingersoll, Tillsonburg, London, and Toronto.

The core obligations:

- **One month's rent maximum** for a last-month rent deposit. Ontario does not allow a separate damage deposit.
- **24 hours' written notice** required before entering a tenant's unit, except in a genuine emergency.
- **Rent increases require a Form N1** and 90 days' written notice, once per 12-month period.
- **Rent control** applies to units first occupied before November 15, 2018. The 2026 guideline is 2.5%. Units first occupied after that date have no cap on the increase amount, but the notice and timing rules still apply.
- **Eviction through the LTB only.** You cannot change the locks, remove belongings, or cut services to pressure a tenant to leave.
- **Standard lease required** for any new tenancy in a private residential rental unit.

If you need a refresher on the [rent increase process and what rent control applies to your unit](/blog/ontario-rent-control-exempt-properties), that post walks through the November 2018 cut-off in detail.

## LTB Access from Oxford County

The Landlord and Tenant Board regional office that handles Oxford County is the London Regional Centre. Hearings are conducted primarily by video. If you file an L1 (eviction for non-payment of rent) for a Woodstock or Tillsonburg property, the case goes through the same London regional pipeline as a London property.

The typical timeline from L1 filing to a hearing order is 8 to 14 weeks in this region. If the tenant doesn't vacate after the 11-day void period expires, enforcement goes through the Sheriff — add another 4 to 8 weeks for that step.

You file all LTB applications online through the Tribunals Ontario portal at tribunalsontario.ca/ltb. Physical filing is available at the London Regional Centre. There is no Oxford County-specific LTB office.

For a full walkthrough of what an LTB hearing involves and how to prepare, see [LTB hearing preparation for Ontario landlords](/blog/ltb-hearing-preparation-ontario-landlords).

## Woodstock Property Standards

The City of Woodstock maintains a Property Standards By-law under the authority of the Ontario Building Code Act. The by-law sets minimum conditions for all residential properties in Woodstock. Enforcement is complaint-driven — a Property Standards Officer responds when a tenant, neighbour, or city inspector files a complaint.

Here is what the Woodstock by-law covers:

**Structural condition**
Walls, floors, ceilings, roofs, stairs, and foundations must be structurally sound and weathertight. Exterior cladding must prevent water infiltration. Broken windows, failing siding, or deteriorating masonry are deficiencies.

**Heating**
The unit must maintain a minimum indoor temperature of 21°C from September 15 to June 1. If your heating system fails during the heating season, this is an urgent deficiency. Under [RTA Section 20](/blog/rta-section-20-maintenance-obligations-ontario), you must repair it promptly — no-heat situations give tenants the right to file a T6 application, and abatements are common.

**Plumbing and hot water**
Hot and cold running water must be available at all times. Hot water must reach a minimum temperature at the tap. The unit must have a functioning toilet, sink, and bath or shower. Drainage must connect to the municipal sewer or an approved septic system.

**Electrical**
All electrical systems must be in safe condition. No exposed wiring, damaged panels, or non-functioning outlets at key fixtures. Smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors must be functional — CO alarms are required near sleeping areas in any unit with a fuel-burning appliance or attached garage.

**Ventilation**
Kitchens and bathrooms must be ventilated to the outside. Basement apartments require adequate air exchange. Condensation problems that result in mould growth can become a bylaw deficiency.

**Pest control**
The unit must be free from rodents, insects, and other pests. If a tenant reports a pest problem, the obligation to treat it falls on the landlord under both the bylaw and the RTA. See [pest control responsibilities for Ontario landlords](/blog/pest-control-ontario-rentals) for a detailed breakdown.

**Exterior and yards**
Property must be maintained in a clean and orderly condition. Broken fences, accumulated debris, deteriorating driveways, and overgrown vegetation can be bylaw deficiencies.

**Enforcement process**
When a complaint is received, the city may contact you first or proceed directly to inspection. If deficiencies are found, the officer issues a Notice of Deficiency or an Order to Comply with a deadline — typically 30 to 90 days depending on severity. If you don't comply, the city can carry out the work and charge you, or proceed with fines. Fines under the Provincial Offences Act can reach $25,000 for a first conviction.

## Ingersoll Property Standards

The Town of Ingersoll operates under its own property standards by-law. The core requirements mirror Woodstock's: structural integrity, heating (minimum 21°C, September to June), plumbing, electrical safety, pest control, and tidy exterior maintenance.

Ingersoll is a smaller municipality — population around 13,000 — with a compact rental market centred on the town core. The enforcement model is the same as Woodstock: complaint-driven, with an Order to Comply process and fine exposure for non-compliance.

One note specific to Ingersoll and other smaller Oxford County municipalities: the building permit and inspection capacity in smaller towns can mean longer lead times for second unit approvals. If you are planning to add a basement apartment or accessory unit in Ingersoll, budget more time for the permit and inspection process than you would in Woodstock or London.

## Tillsonburg Property Standards

The Town of Tillsonburg maintains property standards enforcement with the same provincial framework. Minimum conditions for structural integrity, heating, plumbing, electrical, and pest control apply. The town's enforcement team responds to complaints.

Tillsonburg serves a mixed demographic — retirees, agricultural workers, and families — and has seen growth from manufacturing and logistics employers in the area. The rental market is tighter than in Ingersoll, with low vacancy and steady demand from workers relocating for employment.

For landlords in Tillsonburg: the same RTA obligations apply as anywhere in Ontario, and the LTB process runs through the London Regional Centre. The entry-notice rules, eviction procedure, and lease documentation requirements are unchanged. See [landlord entry rights in Ontario](/blog/landlord-entry-rights-ontario) if you have questions about the 24-hour written notice requirement.

## Second Units and Basement Apartments in Oxford County

Provincial legislation — specifically the More Homes Built Faster Act — requires municipalities to permit up to three residential units on most residential lots with municipal water and sewer services. This applies in Woodstock, Ingersoll, and Tillsonburg, meaning a second unit (basement apartment, garden suite, or additional internal unit) can generally be added as-of-right to a single-family lot with services.

The key requirements for a legal second unit in Oxford County:

**Building permit required**
Any new second unit needs a building permit from the local municipality. The permit process confirms compliance with the Ontario Building Code — ceiling heights, egress windows, fire separation, structural changes.

**Fire Code compliance**
Basement apartments require interconnected smoke alarms on every level, carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas, and proper fire separation between the main unit and the second unit. See [fire safety requirements for Ontario landlords](/blog/fire-safety-ontario-landlords) for the specific requirements.

**Egress windows**
Every bedroom in a basement apartment must have a window large enough to serve as an emergency exit. The minimum opening is 0.35 m² with no dimension less than 380 mm. This is one of the most common deficiencies found during second-unit inspections.

**No registration certificate**
Unlike London, Oxford County municipalities do not have a second unit registration program as a separate step. The building permit and inspection process is the compliance gateway. When the permit is closed after a satisfactory final inspection, you have a legal second unit.

**Rental Housing Licence**
Not required in Woodstock, Ingersoll, or Tillsonburg. No annual licence renewal.

What this means in practice: adding a basement apartment in Oxford County is simpler from a registration standpoint than in London. You need a building permit and a successful final inspection — not a separate registration certificate, a city-specific application, and an annual licensing renewal. The building code and fire code requirements are the same provincially.

## Rent Control and Oxford County New Construction

If your Oxford County property first had a residential unit occupied on or after November 15, 2018, it is exempt from rent control under RTA Section 6.1. This applies to new construction and to units that were newly created after that date — a basement apartment first rented in 2020 is exempt regardless of when the house was built.

Exempt units mean you can increase rent to any amount on lease renewal — but the procedural rules still apply. You need a Form N1, 90 days' notice, and you can only increase once per 12-month period. The absence of a guideline cap does not mean you can skip the notice process. See [Ontario rent control exempt properties](/blog/ontario-rent-control-exempt-properties) for the full breakdown.

## Landlord Record-Keeping in Oxford County

Whether you rent in Woodstock or Tillsonburg, your record-keeping obligations are the same as any Ontario landlord. You should maintain:

- A copy of every signed lease
- A written record of the last-month rent deposit amount and date received
- Annual deposit interest calculations (the rent increase guideline rate each year)
- Written entry notices (keep the originals)
- Move-in and move-out inspection reports with photos
- All written maintenance requests from tenants and your responses
- N1 notices served, with proof of service
- All LTB correspondence and filings

The LTB expects landlords to produce these records at a hearing. The absence of a move-in inspection report, in particular, makes an L10 damage recovery claim extremely difficult after the tenancy ends. See [landlord record-keeping obligations in Ontario](/blog/landlord-record-keeping-ontario) for a documentation checklist.

## Common Mistakes for Oxford County Landlords

**1. Assuming no licensing means no scrutiny**
No rental licence requirement does not mean you can operate without meeting property standards. A single tenant complaint triggers an inspection. Keep your units in good repair before that happens.

**2. Skipping the building permit for a basement apartment**
An unpermitted second unit creates three problems: fire safety risk, insurance exclusion risk (most policies require a legal unit), and difficulty at the LTB if the tenant argues the unit is non-compliant. Get the permit.

**3. Missing the egress window requirement**
This is the most common reason a second-unit inspection fails. Measure your basement bedroom windows before you start the permit process. If the window doesn't meet the minimum opening, factor a window well and new window into the project budget.

**4. Not tracking rent control status per unit**
Oxford County landlords with older properties and newer additions often have a mix: a main unit occupied before 2018 (rent-controlled) and a basement apartment first occupied after 2018 (exempt). Track each unit separately. Applying guideline-limited increases to an exempt unit leaves money uncaptured; applying no-cap increases to a rent-controlled unit creates LTB liability.

**5. Informal lease arrangements**
The Ontario Standard Lease is mandatory for new private residential tenancies. A verbal tenancy or a homemade document that conflicts with the Standard Lease creates disputes. Use the current standard form for every new tenancy.

**6. Not paying deposit interest**
The last-month rent deposit earns interest at the rent increase guideline rate each year. Oxford County landlords who have held a deposit for several years without paying interest owe accumulated amounts. A tenant can recover this via a T1 application. Apply a rent credit or cash payment at each lease anniversary.

**7. Attempting self-help evictions**
No matter how difficult the situation — a tenant who has stopped paying, damaged the property, or simply refuses to leave — the eviction process runs through the LTB. Changing locks, removing belongings, or cutting utilities exposes you to T2 applications, rent abatement, and fines up to $25,000. See [what to do if a tenant stops paying rent in Ontario](/blog/what-to-do-if-tenant-stops-paying-rent-ontario) for the correct N4 → L1 → LTB pipeline.

## Oxford County Rental Market Context

Woodstock is the largest rental market in Oxford County. The city's growth in automotive manufacturing and logistics — Toyota's Canadian operations are a major employer — has sustained consistent rental demand. Bachelor and one-bedroom units in Woodstock run roughly $1,100–$1,400/month. Two-bedroom units typically achieve $1,500–$1,900/month. These are lower than London but vacancy rates are similar — below 2% in recent years.

Ingersoll and Tillsonburg are smaller markets with limited rental supply. That can mean longer tenancies (less turnover) and steady demand from local employment. It can also mean longer vacancy periods when a unit does turn over, because the pool of qualified applicants is smaller. Pricing units appropriately for the local market matters more in smaller communities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a rental licence to rent property in Woodstock?**
No. As of 2026, the City of Woodstock does not have a rental housing licensing program. Property standards enforcement is complaint-driven. You still need to comply with the by-law and the RTA.

**Who do I call if a tenant in Tillsonburg falls behind on rent?**
You serve an N4 notice (Notice to End Tenancy Early for Non-Payment of Rent). If the tenant does not pay or vacate within the 14-day void period, you file an L1 application with the LTB. The file goes to the London Regional Centre. Hearings are by video. The timeline is typically 8 to 14 weeks from filing to an order.

**Is a basement apartment in Ingersoll legal without a permit?**
No. Any secondary unit requires a building permit from the Town of Ingersoll and a final inspection confirming compliance with the Building Code and Fire Code. Operating without a permit creates insurance, LTB, and enforcement exposure.

**Can I increase rent on a Woodstock property first occupied in 2022 to any amount?**
Yes, with limits on process. Units first occupied after November 15, 2018, are exempt from the rent increase guideline. You can increase by any amount, but you must still serve a Form N1 with 90 days' notice and you can only increase once per 12-month period.

**Where is the LTB office for Oxford County landlords?**
The London Regional Centre handles Oxford County. Hearings are conducted by video through the Tribunals Ontario portal. You file all applications online at tribunalsontario.ca/ltb.

**What is the minimum heating temperature I must maintain in Oxford County?**
Under provincial standards and local by-laws, you must maintain a minimum of 21°C from approximately September to June. Failure to maintain heat is an urgent deficiency under both the by-law and RTA Section 20, and tenants can file T6 applications.

**Do Oxford County property standards bylaws interact with the RTA?**
Yes. A tenant can pursue a property standards complaint through the municipality and an RTA maintenance application through the LTB simultaneously. These are parallel processes. A bylaw Order to Comply does not prevent a T6 application, and an LTB maintenance order does not close the bylaw file.

**Can a Woodstock condo board evict my tenant?**
No. Only the Landlord and Tenant Board can order a tenancy terminated. If you rent a condo unit in Woodstock and the condo corporation has concerns about your tenant, they contact you — not the tenant — and you are responsible for addressing compliance issues under the RTA's grounds for eviction.
