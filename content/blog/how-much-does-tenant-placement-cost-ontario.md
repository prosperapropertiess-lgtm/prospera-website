You have a vacant unit and you need a good tenant in it — fast. A property management company says they can handle the whole process. You want to know what that costs before you commit.

The short answer: most tenant placement services in Ontario charge between 50% and 100% of the first month's rent, or a flat fee in the $800–$1,500 range. On a typical London two-bedroom renting at $2,100 per month, you're looking at $1,050 to $2,100 for a quality placement service.

But the fee is only part of the story. What's included, what's excluded, and what happens if the placement doesn't work out — that's where services differ significantly.

This guide explains how tenant placement fees are structured, what you should expect for your money, and how to evaluate whether the cost is worth it in your situation.

---

## The Two Main Pricing Models

Tenant placement services in Ontario use two pricing structures.

### Model 1: Percentage of First Month's Rent

The most common model in the province. The company charges a percentage of the first full month's rent once a tenant is placed and the lease is signed.

- **50% of first month's rent** — typically a lighter-touch service. Listings, showings, and application screening. You review applications and make the final decision.
- **75–100% of first month's rent** — full-service placement. The company handles everything from listing to lease signing, including credit checks, employment verification, reference calls, and move-in inspection.

On a $2,100/month London two-bedroom:
| Fee Rate | Cost |
|---|---|
| 50% | $1,050 |
| 75% | $1,575 |
| 100% | $2,100 |

### Model 2: Flat Fee

Some services charge a fixed amount regardless of the rent level. You'll see ranges from $800 to $1,500 for most residential units. Flat-fee services tend to work well for lower-rent units where a percentage model would be uneconomical.

For a $1,400 bachelor apartment, a 100% fee model charges $1,400. A flat fee of $900 is a better deal for the same scope of work.

For a $2,800 three-bedroom, a flat fee of $1,200 is a discount on what a percentage model would charge ($2,100–$2,800). In this case, the percentage model may reflect more effort on the company's part — better applicant pool, more thorough screening.

**What matters more than the model:** what the fee actually includes.

## What a Full-Service Tenant Placement Fee Should Include

A properly scoped tenant placement service covers the entire process from listing to lease. Here is what you should expect for a fee at or near 100% of first month's rent:

**Listing and marketing:**
- Professional listing copy written to Human Rights Code standards
- Listing posted on REALTOR.ca, Kijiji, Zumper, and relevant London-specific platforms
- Photography direction or review (some services include photography; confirm before signing)

**Inquiry management:**
- Responding to all inquiries within business hours
- Prequalifying applicants by income requirement, timing, and unit fit
- Scheduling and managing showings (in-person and/or virtual)

**Screening:**
- Credit check via Equifax or TransUnion (hard or soft pull depending on process)
- Employment and income verification (pay stubs, employer letters)
- Reference checks from prior landlords (not just a quick call — a real conversation)
- Screening for red flags: [inconsistent income, recent eviction history, incomplete applications](/blog/tenant-screening-red-flags)

**Lease and documentation:**
- Preparation of the Ontario Standard Lease Agreement
- Collection of the last-month rent deposit
- Move-in inspection with photographs

**Not all services include all of the above.** Some exclude photography, some pass the final screening decision to you, and some stop at the signed lease without doing a move-in inspection. Read the service agreement carefully and ask exactly where their work ends.

## What Is Usually NOT Included

Even a full-service tenant placement fee typically does not cover ongoing management after move-in. The placement fee is a one-time transaction: place a tenant, get paid, done.

Things that require a separate arrangement:
- Monthly rent collection and disbursement
- Maintenance coordination and contractor management
- Lease renewal handling and rent increase notices
- Noise or neighbour complaints
- LTB applications if the tenancy goes sideways
- Annual rental unit licence renewal (City of London requirement — see [rental unit licence obligations](/blog/city-of-london-rental-unit-licence))

If you want those covered, you need full property management — not placement-only. The distinction matters significantly. See [tenant placement vs. property management](/blog/tenant-placement-vs-property-management-ontario) for a full comparison of what each service covers.

## The 90-Day Replacement Guarantee

Not every placement service offers one. If the tenant placed by the company leaves, is evicted, or breaks the lease within a defined window — typically 60 to 90 days — does the company replace them, and at what cost?

Some services offer a replacement guarantee: if the tenancy ends within 90 days for a reason within the company's control (bad screening, inadequate verification), they re-place at no charge or at a reduced fee.

Ask for this explicitly before signing a placement agreement. A company confident in its screening process will offer it. One that isn't may be vague about replacement terms.

This matters because one bad tenant placement can cost you far more than the original placement fee — in vacancy time, cleaning and repair costs, and potentially an LTB application. See [what to do if a tenant stops paying rent](/blog/what-to-do-if-tenant-stops-paying-rent-ontario) for what that process looks like and what it costs in time and money.

## Is Tenant Placement Worth the Cost?

The placement fee pays for itself if it reduces your vacancy by even one to two weeks and screens out one problem applicant.

Here is the math for a London two-bedroom at $2,100/month:

**Vacancy cost:**
- Each week of vacancy = $525 in lost rent
- A 4-week DIY vacancy vs. a 2-week professional placement = $1,050 in saved rent
- A 6-week DIY vacancy vs. a 2-week professional placement = $2,100 in saved rent

**Screening error cost:**
- A tenant who stops paying rent after two months triggers an N4, L1, LTB hearing, and Sheriff enforcement — a 5-to-7 month process in London/Middlesex
- At $2,100/month, that's $10,500 to $14,700 in unpaid rent and vacancy
- One thorough screening that catches an applicant who would have defaulted is worth more than the entire placement fee many times over

The placement fee — even at 100% of first month's rent — is not the largest financial risk in a tenancy. The tenant you place is.

## How to Evaluate a Tenant Placement Service

Price is the starting point, not the deciding factor. Ask these questions before signing:

**1. What is your screening process?**
A real answer includes credit check methodology, how they verify income (not just "we ask for pay stubs"), and whether they actually call prior landlords.

**2. Do you have a replacement guarantee, and what triggers it?**
Get the exact timeframe and the conditions in writing.

**3. Who makes the final decision on the tenant?**
Some services present you with a ranked shortlist. Others make the decision themselves. Know which model you're getting.

**4. What documentation do you provide?**
You should receive: the signed Ontario Standard Lease, credit report, screening notes, last month's rent deposit receipt, and move-in inspection report with photos.

**5. What platforms do you list on?**
REALTOR.ca, Zumper, Kijiji, and Facebook Marketplace cover most of London's active rental search. If a service lists only on one platform, they're not working the market.

**6. How quickly do you respond to inquiries?**
Speed matters. A qualified applicant who waits 24 hours for a response from you will have signed a lease with someone else. Ask about response time commitments.

## 7 Common Mistakes When Hiring a Tenant Placement Service

**1. Choosing the cheapest option without confirming what's included**
A 50% fee that excludes credit checks, reference calls, and move-in inspection is not a deal. You're paying for paperwork and listings, not a real screening.

**2. Not asking about the replacement guarantee**
If the company hedges when you ask, that tells you something about how confident they are in their own process.

**3. Assuming placement is ongoing management**
Placement ends at move-in. If something goes wrong in month three — late rent, maintenance issue, noise complaint — you are on your own unless you have a management agreement in place.

**4. Skipping the move-in inspection**
A placement service that doesn't include a documented move-in inspection with photographs is leaving you exposed. Without move-in documentation, you have no baseline to claim deductions from the last month's rent deposit at move-out. See [move-in and move-out inspection requirements](/blog/move-in-move-out-inspection-ontario).

**5. Not verifying the service agreement is compliant with Ontario's Human Rights Code**
Listing descriptions, showing practices, and screening criteria must not discriminate on protected grounds. A company that asks you to describe your "ideal tenant" in demographic terms is exposing you to a human rights complaint.

**6. Treating all placement companies as interchangeable**
Screening depth varies significantly between services. A thorough reference call with a prior landlord surfaces information that a credit score alone will not. Ask specifically how they verify prior tenancy history.

**7. Not comparing placement against vacancy cost**
Landlords who reject a $1,500 placement fee to save money often lose more than that in extra vacancy weeks. Calculate your actual weekly vacancy cost before deciding the fee isn't worth it.

## FAQ

**How much does tenant placement typically cost in Ontario?**
Between 50% and 100% of the first month's rent, or a flat fee of $800–$1,500 for residential units. Full-service placement in London, Ontario typically runs $1,050–$2,100 on a two-bedroom unit at $2,100/month.

**Is the tenant placement fee a one-time charge?**
Yes. A placement fee covers a single tenancy placement. If that tenant leaves and you need a new one, you pay another placement fee — unless a replacement guarantee covers the situation.

**Can I negotiate the placement fee?**
Sometimes, especially on multiple units or ongoing volume. A single unit with a straightforward lease is less likely to get a discount than a landlord who offers a portfolio or a preferred referral arrangement.

**Does the placement fee cover the cost of the credit check?**
At 75–100% of first month's rent, the credit check cost is typically absorbed in the fee. At lower-tier pricing, confirm whether credit check costs are passed through separately.

**What is a 90-day placement guarantee?**
If the placed tenant vacates, is evicted, or breaks the lease within 90 days of move-in, the company re-places the unit at no charge or at a reduced fee. Not all services offer this — ask explicitly before signing.

**Is tenant placement taxable? Does HST apply?**
Tenant placement is a service provided by a property management company, not a residential rental. The placement fee itself is subject to HST. Your gross rent payments as a landlord are exempt from HST. See [HST on residential rental property in Ontario](/blog/hst-residential-rental-property-ontario) for full details.

**What happens if the tenant placed by the company stops paying rent?**
The placement company's responsibility typically ends at move-in. If the tenant defaults, you handle the N4/L1/LTB process yourself — or engage a management company to handle it. A quality screening process is your best protection against this scenario.

**How do I know if my placement service is doing thorough screening?**
Ask for the screening report after placement. It should include the credit report, employment verification documentation, and written screening notes including what the prior landlord said on reference. If they can't provide this, their screening process is not as thorough as advertised.
