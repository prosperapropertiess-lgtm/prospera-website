If you rent out individual rooms in a house—where tenants share a kitchen, bathroom, or common areas—you are operating a rooming house. The City of London treats that differently from a self-contained apartment.

A standard Rental Housing Licence covers self-contained units. A rooming house needs a separate Rooming House Licence. These are two distinct programs. Many London landlords who operate rooming houses don't know this distinction exists, and they operate without the right licence.

This guide explains what a rooming house is, who needs a Rooming House Licence, how to apply, what the city inspects, and how this licence sits alongside the Residential Tenancies Act.

---

## What Counts as a Rooming House in London?

A rooming house is a residential building where three or more people—who are not members of the same family—each rent individual rooms. They share at least one common facility, such as a kitchen, bathroom, or living area.

This is different from a self-contained apartment or unit. A self-contained unit has its own private kitchen and bathroom. Tenants in a self-contained unit do not share those facilities with other tenants.

If your tenants each have their own bedroom but share a kitchen and bathroom with one another, you are running a rooming house.

Common examples in London:

- A house near Western University where four students each rent a bedroom and share the kitchen, two bathrooms, and living room
- An older converted house in East London where five tenants each have a room and share one common bathroom
- A multi-bedroom property in Old North where rooms are rented individually on separate lease agreements

If you are renting a full self-contained unit to a single tenant or household—even if it is in a large house—that is not a rooming house. That falls under the standard Rental Housing Licence program.

## The Rooming House Licence vs. the Rental Housing Licence

This is the distinction most London landlords miss.

The City of London's Rental Housing Licensing program covers self-contained rental units—apartments, basement suites, main-floor units—where each tenant has their own kitchen and bathroom. If you have a duplex or a basement apartment, you need a Rental Housing Licence for each self-contained unit.

The Rooming House Licence covers buildings where multiple tenants share common facilities. It is a separate licence category with different application requirements, different inspection standards, and different ongoing obligations.

You cannot substitute one for the other. Operating a rooming house under a Rental Housing Licence—or operating without any licence at all—puts you in violation of the city's Licensing and Regulatory Services By-law.

The two licences can coexist on the same property in limited situations. For example, a house that has two self-contained units on the upper floors and three rented rooms with shared common areas on the main floor would need both a Rental Housing Licence for the upper units and a Rooming House Licence for the main floor arrangement.

If you are unsure which licence you need, contact the City of London's Business Licensing division before you accept any tenants.

## Zoning: R3 or Higher Is Required

Before you apply for a Rooming House Licence, you need to confirm your property is in a zone that permits this use.

The City of London's Zoning By-law Z.-1 restricts rooming houses to R3 zones and higher—multi-family residential zones. Standard R1 and R2 lots do not permit rooming houses.

This matters. A single-family home in an R1 zone cannot legally operate as a rooming house, regardless of how the building is used. The More Homes Built Faster Act's as-of-right three-unit rule applies to dwelling units (self-contained), not to rooming house room rentals.

You can check your property's zoning through the City of London's online mapping tool at london.ca. Enter your address and look for the zone designation.

If your property is in R3 or higher, you are in the right zone to apply. If it is in R1 or R2, you would need a zoning amendment before proceeding—a process that is costly, time-consuming, and uncertain.

Neighbourhoods near Western University, Old North, and parts of East London tend to have higher R3+ density. These are the areas where rooming houses legally operate in London.

## Who Needs a Rooming House Licence

You need a Rooming House Licence if:

- Your building is in an R3 or higher zone in London
- Three or more tenants each rent individual rooms
- Those tenants share at least one common area or facility
- You are receiving rent from those tenants

The licence must be obtained before you accept tenants. You cannot backfill it after the fact.

If you currently operate a rooming house without this licence, you are in violation of the city by-law. The risks include fines, an order to cease operations, and complications with any future LTB proceedings.

## How to Apply: Step by Step

**Step 1: Confirm zoning**

Verify your property is in an R3 or higher zone using the City of London's online zoning map. This is a prerequisite. The city will not issue a Rooming House Licence for a property in an incompatible zone.

**Step 2: Review the application requirements**

Contact the City of London's Business Licensing division to get the current application package. Requirements include the application form, payment of the licence fee, and documentation confirming the property's ownership or authorization to operate.

**Step 3: Submit for a city inspection**

A Rooming House Licence requires a property inspection before the licence is issued. The inspection is conducted by the city's Property Standards and Business Licensing staff. It covers fire safety, sleeping room sizes, common area conditions, plumbing, electrical, heating, and ventilation.

You must schedule this inspection through the city. Do not accept tenants before the inspection is complete and the licence is issued.

**Step 4: Address any deficiencies**

If the inspection identifies items that do not meet the city's standards, you will receive a list of required corrections before the licence can be issued. Common items include smoke and carbon monoxide alarm placement, egress requirements, and minimum sleeping room dimensions.

**Step 5: Receive your licence**

Once the inspection passes and the fee is paid, the city issues the Rooming House Licence. Post it at the property as required. Licences are renewed annually.

**Step 6: Notify tenants**

Each tenant in the rooming house should receive a copy of their individual room lease agreement. The Ontario Standard Lease applies to rooming house tenancies. See [How to Write a Lease Agreement in Ontario](/blog/how-to-write-lease-agreement-ontario) for the requirements.

## What the City Inspects

The Rooming House Licence inspection is more involved than the standard Rental Housing Licence inspection. Inspectors assess:

**Fire safety**
- Smoke alarms in every sleeping room and on every floor
- Carbon monoxide alarms where required by the Fire Code
- Clear egress from every room (door to the outside or to a corridor leading to the outside)
- Interconnected smoke alarms in buildings with multiple floors
- Proper fire separation between floors and between units where applicable

**Sleeping room requirements**
- Minimum floor area per sleeping room (the Fire Code and Property Standards By-law set minimum dimensions)
- Adequate ceiling height
- At least one window in every sleeping room for natural light and ventilation

**Common areas**
- Adequate shared kitchen and bathroom facilities
- Proper ventilation in bathrooms
- Heating throughout the building (21°C minimum from September 1 to June 15)

**Plumbing and electrical**
- Running hot and cold water in kitchen and bathrooms
- Electrical system in safe working condition
- No exposed wiring, overloaded circuits, or non-compliant panels

**General condition**
- Structural soundness
- No pest infestation
- Exterior and common areas maintained to city property standards

Review the [City of London Property Standards By-law guide](/blog/london-ontario-property-standards-bylaw) before your inspection. Property standards violations can delay your licence or result in an Order to Comply that must be cleared before you can operate.

## Ongoing Obligations After You Get the Licence

Receiving the Rooming House Licence is not the end of your obligations. It is the beginning.

**Annual renewal**
The Rooming House Licence renews each year. The city sends a renewal notice. You must renew before your licence expires. An expired licence means you are operating unlicensed. See [City of London Rental Unit Licence Renewal](/blog/city-of-london-rental-unit-licence-renewal) for how the city's renewal cycle works—the same general principles apply to rooming house renewals.

**Ongoing inspections**
The city may conduct inspections during the licence year, particularly if there are complaints. Property Standards officers can inspect on 24 hours' written notice. Keep the property in compliance with the by-law at all times—not only at renewal.

**Maintaining required standards**
You are required to maintain the property to the same standards that the initial inspection assessed. This includes smoke alarms, sleeping room conditions, heating, plumbing, and common areas. If a smoke alarm is damaged or removed, replace it immediately.

**Keeping licence contact information current**
The licence is tied to your contact information. If you move or change your phone number, update it with the city before renewal notices go out. Out-of-town or hard-to-reach landlords miss renewals because their contact information is outdated.

**Record-keeping**
Keep records of your licence, inspection reports, renewal notices, tenant agreements, and maintenance activity. If a complaint is made or a property standards inspection is triggered, your records are your first line of defence. See [Landlord Record-Keeping in Ontario](/blog/landlord-record-keeping-ontario) for the standard you should maintain.

## How the Rooming House Licence Interacts with the RTA

The Rooming House Licence is a municipal licensing requirement. The Residential Tenancies Act is provincial law. They operate in parallel.

Most rooming house tenants in Ontario are covered by the RTA. This means:

- Each tenant has a tenancy agreement (the Ontario Standard Lease applies)
- You need proper notice for entry into their room (24 hours written notice, RTA Section 27)
- You must maintain the property in good repair under RTA Section 20
- Evictions must follow the RTA process (N4, N5, etc.)—you cannot ask a rooming house tenant to leave simply by verbal notice, even if they are on a month-to-month agreement

Having a Rooming House Licence does not change these tenant rights.

**Entry rights in rooming houses**

Landlord entry rights in rooming houses are nuanced. Each tenant has a right to exclusive use of their own room. You need 24 hours written notice to enter their room for most purposes, the same as for any tenanted unit. You can enter common areas without notice since those are not part of any single tenant's exclusive possession.

**Shared facilities are still your responsibility**

The kitchen, bathrooms, common areas, and shared appliances are your maintenance responsibility under RTA Section 20. If the shared hot water heater fails, you must repair it on the same standard as any landlord obligation—promptly, without unreasonable delay. See [RTA Section 20: Landlord Maintenance Obligations](/blog/rta-section-20-maintenance-obligations-ontario) for the repair timelines.

**Inter-tenant conflicts**

When one tenant's behaviour interferes with another tenant's reasonable enjoyment of the property, you have obligations under RTA Section 22. Ignoring persistent noise complaints between rooming house tenants can expose you to T2 applications from the affected tenant. This is a common issue in student rooming houses near Western.

**The LTB for rooming house disputes**

Rooming house tenants file LTB applications just like any other tenant. The London Regional LTB office handles these cases. L1 applications for rent arrears and T6 maintenance applications are both common in rooming house contexts. For more on the LTB process, see [LTB Hearing Preparation for Ontario Landlords](/blog/ltb-hearing-preparation-ontario-landlords).

## Insurance for Rooming Houses

Standard homeowner or landlord insurance does not automatically cover a rooming house. You need to tell your insurer exactly what you are operating. A rooming house has a different risk profile—more tenants, more shared facilities, higher turnover, greater wear on common areas—and your policy must reflect that.

Failing to disclose that you operate a rooming house can void your policy if a claim is made. See [Rental Property Insurance in Ontario](/blog/rental-property-insurance-ontario) for the basics of landlord insurance coverage. Confirm with your broker that your policy explicitly covers a multi-tenant rooming house arrangement.

## 7 Common Mistakes Rooming House Landlords Make

**1. Applying for the wrong licence**
Getting a Rental Housing Licence when you need a Rooming House Licence—or vice versa—is not a minor paperwork error. The city's licensing program is distinct between the two. Operating with the wrong licence is the same as operating without one for compliance purposes.

**2. Missing the zoning check**
Assuming R1 or R2 zoning is fine because "it's just rooms." It is not. Rooming houses require R3 or higher. Check the zoning map before spending money on the property or renovations.

**3. Accepting tenants before the licence is issued**
You cannot operate a rooming house while waiting for the licence. The licence must be in hand before tenants move in. Accepting rent before this point starts the tenancy under non-compliant conditions.

**4. Using one lease for all tenants**
Each tenant in a rooming house has a separate tenancy. Each needs their own Ontario Standard Lease, with the rent amount, start date, and terms specific to them. A single lease covering the whole house does not satisfy this requirement.

**5. Ignoring maintenance on shared facilities**
The shared kitchen appliances, common bathrooms, and hallways are your responsibility. These are not negotiable terms in the lease. The RTA requires you to maintain them, and tenants can file T6 applications if they are not maintained.

**6. Letting smoke alarms go unchecked**
Fire safety in rooming houses is taken seriously. Each sleeping room needs a smoke alarm. Interconnected alarms are required in multi-storey buildings. An inspector who finds a missing or dead smoke alarm during a complaint-triggered visit will issue an Order to Comply immediately.

**7. Not tracking individual deposit amounts**
Every tenant's last-month-rent deposit is tracked separately. If one tenant's rent increases, their deposit top-up obligation is separate from the others. Keep individual records for each tenancy. See [What Can a Landlord Deduct from Last Month's Rent in Ontario](/blog/what-can-landlord-deduct-from-last-month-rent-ontario) for the deposit rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a rooming house the same as a boarding house?**
Not exactly. A boarding house typically includes meals. A rooming house (or multi-tenant house) rents rooms only, with tenants responsible for their own food and cooking in shared facilities. The distinction matters because providing regular meals can change how the relationship is classified under provincial law. If you provide meals, speak with a lawyer before proceeding.

**Do rooming house tenants have full RTA protections?**
Yes, in almost all cases. Most rooming house tenants in Ontario are covered by the RTA in the same way as apartment tenants. They have the right to proper notice, maintenance standards, and protection from illegal eviction. The RTA carves out very limited exceptions (such as a person sharing a kitchen with the homeowner who also lives there), but most standard rooming house arrangements are fully covered.

**Can I operate a rooming house in an R2 zone?**
No. The City of London's Zoning By-law Z.-1 restricts rooming houses to R3 and higher zones. An R2 property cannot be licensed as a rooming house regardless of how it is used. You would need a zoning amendment—a formal process through the city's planning department—before this would be possible.

**What happens if I operate without a Rooming House Licence?**
The City of London can issue fines for unlicensed operation. You may also receive an Order to Cease operation. Beyond city enforcement, an unlicensed operation creates complications at the LTB—a tenant who raises your licensing non-compliance at a hearing can use it to challenge an eviction application. It also creates insurance exposure.

**How much does the Rooming House Licence cost?**
Contact the City of London's Business Licensing division for the current fee schedule. Licence fees are set annually and are higher than the standard Rental Housing Licence fee due to the more intensive inspection requirements. Budget for the licence fee plus any costs to bring the property to inspection standards.

**How is the Rooming House Licence renewal different from the Rental Housing Licence renewal?**
The renewal cycle works similarly—the city sends a notice, you renew before the expiry date, and a re-inspection may be required depending on complaint history or city program requirements. The key difference is that rooming houses face more scrutiny due to the shared-facility arrangement and higher occupancy density.

**Do I need a separate Rental Housing Licence if my property also has a self-contained suite?**
Yes. If your property has both a rooming house arrangement and a self-contained suite (with its own kitchen and bathroom), the self-contained suite requires a Rental Housing Licence. The Rooming House Licence covers only the rooming house portion. Contact the city to confirm your situation before applying.

**Can I convert my rooming house to individual self-contained units?**
You can, but it requires building permits, compliance with the Ontario Building Code for each new unit (separate kitchen, bathroom, sleeping area), and potentially Second Unit Registration and a Rental Housing Licence for the resulting units. See [City of London Second Unit Registration](/blog/city-of-london-second-unit-registration) for how that process works. Converting to self-contained units is typically the route landlords take when they want to move away from the rooming house licensing requirements.
