## What Residential Property Management Actually Covers

The term gets used loosely, so it's worth being precise. Residential property management means overseeing residential rental units — houses, townhomes, condos, duplexes, and small multi-unit buildings — on behalf of the property owner.

It is distinct from commercial property management, which deals with office buildings, retail, and industrial space. The rules, tenant relationships, and legal frameworks are entirely different. In Ontario, residential rentals fall under the **Residential Tenancies Act (RTA)**, which governs everything from how you raise rent to how you can end a tenancy.

A full-service residential property manager typically handles:

- **Tenant sourcing and screening** — listing the unit, showing it, verifying applications, running credit and background checks
- **Lease execution** — preparing and signing the Ontario Standard Lease
- **Rent collection** — automated collection, late payment follow-up, serving N4 notices when needed
- **Maintenance coordination** — handling routine requests, emergency repairs, and contractor relationships
- **Inspections** — move-in, move-out, and periodic condition checks
- **Legal compliance** — ensuring rent increases, notices, and entry procedures follow the RTA
- **Financial reporting** — monthly statements, year-end summaries for tax filing
- **Tenant relations** — responding to disputes, noise complaints, and communication
- **LTB matters** — filing applications and attending hearings if required

If you've tried managing a London rental on your own, you've likely discovered that each of these areas has its own pitfalls. Miss one step on an eviction notice and you're starting over. Raise rent without proper notice and you risk a T1 application from your tenant. The RTA is detailed and unforgiving.

## Why London, Ontario Specifically Changes the Math

London's rental market has its own dynamics that affect what good property management looks like here.

**Student-driven demand is significant.** Western University and Fanshawe College together enroll over 55,000 students. Properties near campus tend to turn over every one to two years, which means more frequent leasing cycles, more inspections, more lease administration. A property manager experienced in London knows how to price, market, and screen for this tenant segment.

**The North End, South London, and downtown core each have different rent ranges and tenant profiles.** A property manager with local expertise knows what a 3-bedroom on Wonderland Road should rent for versus a similar unit in Old East Village — and they'll know the difference matters by hundreds of dollars a month.

**London's LTB backlog is real.** Ontario's Landlord and Tenant Board has faced significant delays across the province. In London, landlords who make procedural errors on notices often find themselves waiting months longer than necessary. A property manager who knows exactly how to file a correct [N4 notice for non-payment of rent](/blog/n4-notice-ontario) the first time saves landlords weeks or months of lost rental income.

**Vacancy rates fluctuate seasonally.** London has distinct rental seasons — summer brings student movers, September is peak demand near Fanshawe. Timing your marketing and lease end dates to align with peak demand is a strategic advantage most self-managing landlords miss.

## What to Look for in a London Residential Property Manager

Not all property managers are equal. In Ontario, property managers who charge fees for their services must be registered with the **Real Estate Council of Ontario (RECO)** under the Trust in Real Estate Services Act (TRESA). Ask for registration before you sign anything.

Beyond credentials, here's what to evaluate:

**1. RTA fluency.** Your property manager should be able to explain notice requirements, rent increase timelines, and the LTB process without hesitation. Ask them: "Walk me through how you'd handle a tenant who's two months behind on rent." The answer should be confident and specific.

**2. Local market knowledge.** They should know London's neighbourhoods, rental price ranges, and seasonal patterns. Vague answers about "the market" are a red flag.

**3. Maintenance network.** Ask who handles emergency repairs and whether you're charged a markup on contractor invoices. Some companies mark up maintenance costs by 10–20%. Others use their volume to get you better rates than you'd find on your own.

**4. Communication standards.** How often will you receive reports? What's their response time on maintenance requests? How do they notify you before spending above a certain threshold? Get these answers in writing.

**5. Tenant screening process.** A property manager's screening process is one of the highest-value things they offer. Ask what they check: credit, employment verification, rental history, references. A weak screening process upfront creates expensive problems downstream. Understanding [tenant screening red flags](/blog/tenant-screening-red-flags) helps you evaluate whether their process matches best practice.

**6. Transparent contracts.** Read the management agreement carefully. Know what fees apply for leasing, renewal, vacancy, maintenance coordination, and early termination. A good property manager should welcome these questions.

## How the Management Process Works After You Sign

Once you engage a residential property manager in London, the onboarding process typically looks like this:

1. **Property assessment** — The manager visits the unit, documents condition, and recommends any immediate repairs or upgrades to improve marketability
2. **Pricing analysis** — They pull current market comps to set an optimal rent rate
3. **Marketing launch** — Professional photos, listings on Rentals.ca, Kijiji, and internal databases
4. **Tenant screening** — Applications reviewed, credit checks run, employment and references verified
5. **Lease signing** — Ontario Standard Lease prepared and executed, move-in date confirmed
6. **Move-in inspection** — Documented condition report with photos signed by tenant
7. **Ongoing management** — Rent collection, maintenance, communication, compliance
8. **Annual review** — Rent increase analysis, lease renewal decisions, property condition update

The landlord's involvement drops significantly after onboarding. For most clients, it becomes monthly statement reviews and occasional decisions on major repairs or capital investments.

## Residential vs. Commercial: Why the Distinction Matters

If you've searched for property management in London and found results that don't seem quite right for a house or duplex, you may have landed on commercial management content. The distinction matters for several reasons:

- Residential tenancies in Ontario are governed by the RTA. Commercial leases are not.
- Residential tenants have strong statutory protections — eviction timelines, rent control rules, notice requirements — that don't apply to commercial tenants.
- The LTB handles residential disputes. Commercial disputes go through civil court.
- Residential property managers need different licensing and experience than commercial managers.

When you're searching for help with a house, duplex, townhome, or apartment in London, you want a manager whose core expertise is residential. That's where the RTA complexity lives, and it's where the right manager earns their fee.
