You serve a Form N1. You give 90 days' notice. The rent increase looks correct. Then a letter arrives: your tenant has filed a T1 application at the Landlord and Tenant Board.

A T1 is a tenant-side LTB application. It is called a *Tenant's Application for a Rent Reduction* or a *Tenant's Application About a Rent Increase*. When a tenant believes you charged rent above what the RTA allows, this is how they recover the overcharge — through an LTB hearing, not through a letter, not through withholding rent, and not through a phone call.

Most landlords are caught off guard by a T1 because they never expected to be a respondent at the LTB. This guide explains what a T1 is, the most common grounds, what landlords owe as respondents, and how to avoid the situations that produce T1 applications in the first place.

---

## What Is a T1 Application?

A T1 is filed by a tenant at the Landlord and Tenant Board when they believe the landlord has charged or is charging rent in excess of what the RTA permits.

The main grounds are:

- **Rent charged above the lawful maximum** — the most common ground. This includes charging above the previous lawful rent, charging above the rent increase guideline, charging above the allowed amount for a rent-controlled unit, or imposing an increase without proper N1 notice.
- **Overcharge of a last-month's rent deposit** — collecting more than one month's rent as a deposit, or collecting a deposit in excess of the lawful rent at the time it was collected.
- **Failure to pay interest on the last-month's rent deposit** — tenants are entitled to annual interest at the guideline rate (see [last-month-rent-interest-ontario](/blog/last-month-rent-interest-ontario)).
- **Illegal charges** — charging fees the RTA does not permit, such as a key deposit above cost, a pet deposit, or a cleaning fee built into the lease.
- **Rent increase without proper notice** — an increase imposed without Form N1, or with insufficient notice.

The T1 can seek:

1. A finding that the charge was unlawful
2. A refund of the overcharge (up to the amount paid, with no maximum on the total)
3. An order that the lawful rent is a specified lower amount going forward
4. Interest on amounts owed

There is a one-year limitation period for most T1 claims — tenants can only recover amounts paid in the 12 months before the application was filed. However, if the unlawful increase is ongoing (the tenant is still being charged above the lawful rent), the LTB can order a prospective correction regardless of when the overcharge began.

## The Rent Control Framework: Why T1 Applications Happen

Understanding T1 applications starts with understanding rent control in Ontario.

**Rent-controlled units** are units first occupied for residential purposes before November 15, 2018. For these units, rent increases are capped at the annual guideline set by the province. The 2026 guideline is 2.5%. You cannot charge above the guideline without an Above Guideline Increase (AGI) order granted by the LTB.

**Exempt units** are units first occupied on or after November 15, 2018. These have no guideline cap. You can raise the rent to any amount — but you still need to give 90 days' written notice using Form N1, and you can only raise the rent once every 12 months.

For a full breakdown, see [ontario-rent-control-exempt-properties](/blog/ontario-rent-control-exempt-properties).

The T1 matters because:

- Landlords who mistake an exempt unit for a rent-controlled unit (or vice versa) charge the wrong amount.
- Landlords who skip the 90-day notice window or use the wrong form charge rent on an invalid increase.
- Landlords who miscalculate the guideline percentage charge above the lawful rent.
- Landlords who apply the rent increase on a different date than authorized create an unlawful overcharge.

Any of these errors can produce a T1.

## The Most Common T1 Scenario: Increase Without Proper Notice

The most common T1 ground is not a deliberate overcharge. It is an N1 procedural error that made the increase invalid.

Common N1 errors that void a rent increase:

**Wrong notice period.** The N1 must be received by the tenant at least 90 days before the new rent takes effect. If you serve it on Day 1 and the increase starts on Day 89, the increase is void. The 90-day clock runs from the date of deemed receipt — not the date you handed over the form or dropped it in the mail.

For mail service, deemed receipt is 5 business days after mailing under RTA s.191. That means if you mail the N1 on January 1, deemed receipt is January 8 (allowing for weekends), and the earliest valid rent increase date is April 7.

If you miscounted, the increase is premature and the tenant is owed a refund of the difference.

**Increase on the wrong date.** Rent can only be increased on a date that is:
- At least 90 days after the N1 was received; and
- At least 12 months after the last rent increase (or the start of the tenancy, if no prior increase).

If your increase date violates either rule, the increase is void — even if the amount was correct.

**Wrong form.** Using a custom letter instead of the provincial Form N1 is not permitted for rent-controlled units and is best practice for exempt units as well. A T1 could be argued on the basis that the notice did not comply with prescribed form requirements.

**Amount above the guideline.** For rent-controlled units, any increase above the guideline (2.5% for 2026) requires a separate AGI application. Without one, the overlimit amount is an unlawful overcharge.

For a step-by-step guide to doing this correctly, see [how-to-increase-rent-ontario-step-by-step](/blog/how-to-increase-rent-ontario-step-by-step).

## What Happens When a T1 Is Filed

When a tenant files a T1 application:

1. The LTB assigns a file number and mails you a Notice of Hearing.
2. The Notice tells you the hearing date, format (video, written, or in-person), and what you need to bring.
3. You have the right to respond, to file documents, and to attend the hearing.

You are the respondent. The tenant is the applicant. The burden is on the tenant to prove the overcharge — but as the respondent, you need to rebut their case with your own documentation.

**What you need to bring:**

- The signed lease agreement showing the lawful rent at the start of the tenancy
- Every Form N1 you have ever served for this unit
- The Certificate of Service for each N1 (how the form was served and when)
- A complete rent ledger showing what was charged and paid each month
- Any AGI order granted by the LTB, if applicable
- Proof that the unit is exempt from rent control, if applicable (occupancy permit or first-tenancy start date documentation)
- Last-month's rent deposit receipt and interest calculation records (see [last-month-rent-interest-ontario](/blog/last-month-rent-interest-ontario))

The LTB adjudicator will determine the lawful rent for the unit and compare it to what was actually charged. If there is a gap, you owe a refund of the difference.

## How to Determine the Lawful Rent for Your Unit

The lawful rent is the highest amount a landlord is entitled to charge under the RTA at any given time. It is calculated as follows:

**Step 1 — Start with the first rent.**
The lawful rent begins at the amount agreed to in the first tenancy agreement for the unit.

**Step 2 — Apply only valid guideline increases.**
For each subsequent year, you may increase the lawful rent by the guideline percentage — but only if:
- You gave at least 90 days' proper N1 notice; and
- At least 12 months passed since the prior increase.

Each missed or invalid increase does NOT increase the lawful rent. If you forgot to give an N1 one year, the lawful rent stays where it was.

**Step 3 — Apply any AGI increase.**
If you received an Above Guideline Increase order from the LTB (see [above-guideline-rent-increase-ontario](/blog/above-guideline-rent-increase-ontario)), the allowable increase from that order is added.

**Step 4 — Account for new tenancy.**
If there was a vacancy between tenants, the new starting rent resets the lawful rent for rent-controlled units under vacancy decontrol rules — the new rent can be freely negotiated at the start of the new tenancy.

**For exempt units:**
The lawful rent is simply whatever amount was agreed to in the current lease, plus any valid N1 increase given since the tenancy started. There is no guideline cap, but the procedural rules still apply.

## The Deposit Overcharge Issue

A T1 can also be filed if the landlord collected a last-month's rent deposit that exceeded the lawful rent at the time of collection.

This can happen when:
- A landlord collects the deposit based on a planned rent amount, but the rent in the signed lease ends up being lower.
- A landlord increases the deposit over time without the tenant's consent.
- A landlord holds a deposit from a previous tenancy and the amount exceeds what was lawfully chargeable.

Under RTA s.106, the deposit cannot exceed one month's lawful rent. If you collected $2,300 as a deposit when the lawful rent was $2,100, you owe the $200 difference back.

The deposit overcharge remedy is typically a refund credit applied to the deposit or a cash payment to the tenant.

---

## Interest on the Last-Month's Rent Deposit

This is a frequent T1 ground and one that surprises landlords who have done everything else correctly.

Under RTA s.106(6), landlords must pay annual interest on the last-month's rent deposit at the guideline rate. If you have held a $2,100 deposit since January 2022 without paying interest, the tenant is entitled to file a T1 and recover the accumulated amount.

As of 2026 (guideline: 2.5%), a landlord holding a $2,100 deposit since January 2022 owes approximately $207–$215 in accumulated interest, depending on the guideline rates applied each year.

The correct approach is to either:
- Apply the interest as a rent credit in the month it comes due (usually on the anniversary of the deposit), or
- Pay the interest in cash.

Both methods satisfy the obligation. Failure to do either leaves you exposed to a T1.

For a full calculation guide, see [last-month-rent-interest-ontario](/blog/last-month-rent-interest-ontario).

## London, St. Thomas, and Strathroy Context

If your rental unit is in London, Middlesex County, or the surrounding area, there are a few local factors that increase T1 exposure:

**The 2018 cut-off and new construction.** London has seen significant new residential construction since 2018. Many downtown condos, stacked townhomes, and purpose-built rental buildings completed after November 15, 2018 are rent-control exempt. Landlords in these buildings sometimes believe they have no rules at all — but the N1 and 90-day notice requirement still applies. A T1 for improper notice is just as valid for an exempt unit.

**University District and student housing.** Student rental landlords in London frequently change rents between tenancies. When the same tenant stays year-over-year (which is more common than many student landlords expect), every rent increase must follow N1 rules. Informal texts or emails announcing a higher rent for the new academic year do not count as proper notice.

**Rental Housing Licence and T1 interaction.** A T1 is an LTB application and operates independently of the City of London Rental Housing Licensing program. However, an outstanding LTB order on a property (including a T1 award) can complicate a licence renewal. See [city-of-london-rental-unit-licence-renewal](/blog/city-of-london-rental-unit-licence-renewal).

**LTB hearing format.** London-area T1 hearings are typically conducted by video since 2021 through the London Regional LTB office. In straightforward overcharge cases, written hearings may be assigned. If you receive a written hearing notice, see [ltb-written-hearing-ontario](/blog/ltb-written-hearing-ontario) for how to prepare your submission.

## Common Mistakes That Produce T1 Applications

**1. Applying the increase before the 90-day notice period expires.**
The most preventable T1 ground. Count from deemed receipt, not from when you handed over the form. When in doubt, give 95+ days.

**2. Calculating the guideline incorrectly.**
The guideline applies to the current lawful rent — not to the base rent in the original lease. If the lawful rent is $1,980 and the guideline is 2.5%, the allowable increase is $49.50 (not $50). Rounding up to $2,030 when you're entitled to $2,029.50 is technically an overcharge.

**3. Increasing rent for a rent-controlled unit above the guideline.**
If you believe costs justify a larger increase, you need an AGI from the LTB — not a larger N1. See [above-guideline-rent-increase-ontario](/blog/above-guideline-rent-increase-ontario).

**4. Never paying deposit interest.**
Most small landlords have never paid deposit interest. If your tenant has been with you for three years and you have never paid the annual interest, a T1 would succeed on this ground alone.

**5. Confusing the "first occupied" date for the rent control cut-off.**
The cut-off is the date the unit was *first occupied for residential purposes*, not the date you bought the property, the date the lease was signed, or the date the building was permitted. A common London scenario: a building with a Certificate of Occupancy from March 2018 is rent-controlled even if you bought it in 2024.

**6. Collecting a deposit above the signed lease amount.**
If the deposit was collected before the final signed rent amount was agreed, make sure the deposit matches the signed lease. Any excess should be refunded or applied as a rent credit immediately.

**7. Not keeping records of past N1 forms.**
When a T1 is filed, you need to show your complete rent history from day one of the tenancy. If you can't document a valid N1 for every increase you have charged, the increase may be treated as unlawful by default.

## What Happens If the LTB Finds in the Tenant's Favour

If the adjudicator determines you charged above the lawful rent:

- You must refund the overcharge. There is no cap on the refund amount — if the overcharge ran for 36 months at $80/month above the lawful rent, the refund is $2,880 plus interest.
- The LTB will set the lawful rent for the unit going forward. This becomes the new legal maximum.
- You may be ordered to pay the tenant's hearing costs in exceptional cases.
- An outstanding LTB order is a matter of record and can affect your Rental Housing Licence renewal in London.

You have the right to review the order if there was a serious error of law or fact (see [ltb-review-order-ontario](/blog/ltb-review-order-ontario)) or to appeal to the Divisional Court within 30 days on a question of law. In most T1 cases, an error in the rent calculation is a question of fact, not law, so a Divisional Court appeal is rarely available.

## T1 Application Quick Reference

| Issue | Tenant's Ground | What You Need to Defend |
|---|---|---|
| Increase above guideline | Rent charged above lawful maximum | Proof unit is exempt, or proof AGI order obtained |
| N1 notice too short | Procedural violation | Served N1 + Certificate of Service with dates |
| Deposit overcharge | Deposit > one month's lawful rent | Original lease + lawful rent at collection date |
| No deposit interest paid | Failure to pay annual interest | Interest calculation + proof of payment |
| Illegal charge in lease | Prohibited fee | No prohibited fees were charged |
| Increase more than once in 12 months | Timing violation | Complete rent increase history with dates |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a tenant file a T1 for a rent increase that happened three years ago?**
Only for the portion of the overcharge paid in the 12 months before the application was filed. If the original invalid increase happened three years ago but the tenant is still paying the inflated rent today, the LTB can order a correction going forward and refund the past 12 months.

**What if I didn't know the unit was rent-controlled?**
Ignorance of the rent control rules is not a defence at the LTB. The adjudicator will determine the lawful rent based on the first-occupied date and the history of valid increases. If overcharges occurred, a refund will be ordered regardless of intent.

**Can I challenge whether the unit is rent-controlled at the T1 hearing?**
Yes. If you believe the unit was first occupied after November 15, 2018 and is therefore exempt, you can produce the occupancy permit and argue the exemption applies. The burden is on you to establish the exemption.

**Does a T1 application affect an eviction proceeding?**
Not directly. A T1 runs on its own track. However, if the tenant raises the T1 claim as a Section 82 issue at an L1 hearing, the adjudicator can hear both applications together. This is relatively uncommon but possible.

**What if the tenant just stopped paying rent instead of filing a T1?**
Withholding rent is not a valid response to a rent overcharge — tenants must pay rent and pursue the T1 remedy separately. If a tenant stops paying rent, you proceed with an N4 and L1 application as normal. See [n4-notice-ontario](/blog/n4-notice-ontario).

**Is there a fee for the tenant to file a T1?**
Yes — the LTB filing fee for a T1 is $53 for most applications. This creates some self-selection — tenants generally file T1 applications when the overcharge is meaningful.

**Can the T1 result in an order to terminate the tenancy?**
No. A T1 is a money remedy and a rent correction order. It does not give the LTB grounds to terminate the tenancy.

**What if I disagree with the T1 outcome?**
You can file a review under RTA s.209 within 30 days if there was a serious error of law, fact, or jurisdiction, or if natural justice was denied. See [ltb-review-order-ontario](/blog/ltb-review-order-ontario). A Divisional Court appeal is available on questions of law only within 30 days of the order.

## How to Prevent T1 Applications

T1 applications are almost entirely preventable. The same documentation system that defends you if one is filed is the system that keeps you from facing one.

**Keep a rent ledger from day one.** Document the starting rent, every N1 served (with served-on date and deemed-receipt date), every increase date, and every deposit payment. This is not a large job — it is a spreadsheet column updated once a year.

**Calculate the lawful rent before serving the N1.** Confirm the guideline rate from ontario.ca, apply it to the current lawful rent (not the base lease rent), and use the official Form N1. File the N1 with a Certificate of Service the same day.

**Pay deposit interest annually.** On the anniversary of the deposit, calculate the interest owing (deposit amount × guideline rate) and apply it as a rent credit or pay it directly. Document it in writing to the tenant.

**Know the rent control status of your unit.** The cut-off is the date of first residential occupancy. If you are not certain, check the Certificate of Occupancy for the building.

Landlords managing multiple properties — or those who are new to the N1 process — often find that having a property manager handle the annual rent increase cycle eliminates T1 risk entirely. A missed deemed-receipt calculation or a missed interest payment on even one unit can produce a T1 award that exceeds the management fee for the year.
