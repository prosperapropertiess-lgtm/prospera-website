Finding off-campus housing near Western University is one of the more stressful parts of starting school in London, Ontario. The rental market around Western moves quickly — units near campus get snapped up months before September, and if you're coming from out of province or another country, trying to find a place without being here in person makes it even harder.

This guide is written for incoming Western students who need straight information about where to live, what things actually cost in 2026, how to find a place without getting scammed, and what to expect from the application process. Whether you're a first-year moving into your first apartment or a grad student relocating from overseas, this covers what you need.

## Where Is Western University, and Why Does Location Matter?

Western's main campus sits in the northwest part of London, bordered by Western Road to the east, Sarnia Road to the north, Wonderland Road to the west, and Oxford Street West to the south. The campus is large — you can walk from one end to the other, but it takes time.

Location matters for off-campus housing because the areas directly surrounding Western are expensive and competitive. The closer you are, the more you pay and the faster units disappear. Move further out and you get more space and lower rent, but you'll be relying on the London Transit Commission (LTC) bus network or a bike to get to campus.

London's transit is improving but it's not like Toronto. A bus trip that looks short on the map can take 25–35 minutes in practice. That's worth knowing before you commit to a lease.

## The Best Neighbourhoods Near Western University

### Broughdale / Old North (Closest to Campus)

Broughdale Avenue is the street directly bordering Western's north side. It's one of the most student-dense streets in London — rows of Victorian houses, rented almost exclusively to students. If you've heard of the Purple Fest or the Broughdale street parties, this is the street.

Living right on Broughdale puts you a five-minute walk from the university gates. It also puts you in the middle of a lot of noise, especially in September and March. Units here fill fast — we're talking January and February for September move-ins. By the time you see a listing in July, you're looking at what's left.

**Old North**, the broader neighbourhood around Broughdale, extends north along Richmond Street and offers slightly quieter streets with the same walking distance to campus. There are a mix of basement apartments, upper units, and full house rentals in older brick homes.

Rent in Old North / Broughdale: rooms in shared houses typically run $800–$1,100/month per person. Full house rentals for 4–6 students often run $3,000–$4,500/month total. Basement apartments range from $1,200–$1,600/month.

Good for: students who want to walk to every class and don't mind a livelier neighbourhood.

### Masonville (North of Campus)

Masonville is north of Western along Richmond Street, centred around the Masonville Place mall. It's more residential and quieter than Broughdale — a mix of apartment buildings, townhouses, and some basement units.

The LTC Route 6 (Richmond) connects Masonville to Western and downtown London, and it runs reasonably often. You're also close to amenities: Masonville has a Whole Foods, a Shoppers Drug Mart, several restaurants, and easy access to Costco on Fanshawe Park Road.

Rent in Masonville tends to be higher for purpose-built apartments because you're in a higher-demand, amenity-rich area. Expect $1,400–$1,900 for a one-bedroom, $1,800–$2,400 for a two-bedroom. Shared houses in the surrounding streets are comparable to Old North.

Good for: students who want quieter surroundings, access to grocery stores, and a modern apartment building rather than a shared house.

### Byron / Riverbend (West of Campus)

Byron sits west of campus, on the other side of Wonderland Road. It's one of London's most established residential neighbourhoods — well-kept houses, mature trees, close to the Thames River. The trade-off is distance: Byron is about a 20-minute bus ride to campus on Route 17.

Rent is often lower here than in Old North because you're further out. Two-bedroom units in Byron can run $1,500–$1,900/month. For students who want space, a quieter environment, and don't mind the commute, it's a legitimate option.

Good for: graduate students, mature students, or anyone who values quiet over proximity.

### Downtown London (Southeast of Campus)

Downtown London is about 25–35 minutes from Western by transit. It's the most walkable area of London overall — restaurants, bars, Covent Garden Market, Victoria Park — and it has real urban density that other parts of the city don't.

Renting downtown gives you more options in purpose-built apartment buildings, and you're well-positioned for bus connections to almost anywhere in the city. You're also close to the nightlife on Richmond Row without being inside the noise.

The commute to Western, though, is real. Plan for it. The Route 6 or Route 2 along Richmond gets you there, but you're looking at 25–30 minutes one way.

Good for: students who want city energy, access to good transit, and a shorter commute to downtown employers (for co-op or part-time work).

### Old East Village (Further Out — Budget Option)

Old East Village, off Dundas Street East, is further from Western than most students choose, but it's worth knowing about as a budget option. Transit to Western is indirect and takes 40+ minutes. But rent is genuinely affordable — basement apartments in the $1,000–$1,300/month range are more common here than anywhere near campus.

If you're cost-conscious, have a flexible schedule, and don't need to be on campus every day (grad students, some programs), it's worth considering.

## What Does Rent Actually Cost Near Western in 2026?

This is what students coming from other cities consistently underestimate. London is cheaper than Toronto or Vancouver, but the blocks immediately around Western are not cheap.

Here's a realistic picture of what you'll find in 2026:

- **Room in a shared student house (4–6 people):** $800–$1,100/month per person, often utilities included
- **Bachelor / studio apartment:** $1,150–$1,500/month
- **One-bedroom apartment:** $1,450–$1,900/month
- **Two-bedroom apartment (split two ways):** $1,800–$2,400/month total, or $900–$1,200 per person
- **Full 4-bedroom student house (split four ways):** $3,000–$4,200/month total, or $750–$1,050 per person
- **Basement apartment:** $1,100–$1,500/month, often with some utilities included

The units that look cheap in listings often have hidden costs. Ask before you view: what utilities are included, what's the average monthly cost for the ones that aren't, and whether internet is separate. A unit at $1,300/month with heat and water included may actually cost you less than a $1,150/month unit where you pay everything separately.

Also: **first and last month's rent is required upfront in Ontario.** That's two months of rent on signing — budget for it. A landlord cannot legally ask for a security deposit or damage deposit on top of first and last. If they do, that extra amount is not permitted under Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act.

## When to Start Looking

This is the most important piece of advice in this guide: **if you're moving in September, start looking in January or February.**

That's not an exaggeration. The houses on Broughdale and the streets around it are often locked up by February for September. Good apartment buildings fill by April. By June, the market is competitive. By August — when most students realize they need housing — you're competing for whatever's left.

If you're searching in August and reading this, don't panic. Units still come available, but you need to move fast. Check daily, respond the same day, and be ready to book a viewing within 24–48 hours of reaching out.

Timeline guide:
- **January–March:** ideal for securing houses and basement apartments close to campus
- **April–May:** still good options, mostly apartments
- **June:** competitive; move quickly
- **July–August:** tight, but not impossible — you need to be fast and flexible
- **September (after move-in weekend):** some units open up as plans fall through

For January intake students, the market is far less competitive. You'll have more options and a normal amount of time to decide.

## How the Rental Application Works in Ontario

Here is what most Western landlords will ask for:

1. **A rental application** — your current and previous addresses, employment or income information, and emergency contacts.
2. **Government-issued photo ID** — passport or driver's licence. Permanent residents and international students: your passport and study permit are fine.
3. **Proof of income or enrollment** — your Western acceptance letter, a scholarship confirmation, an OSAP summary, an employment offer letter, or documentation of financial support from family. If you have a part-time job, pay stubs help.
4. **A credit check** — the landlord may request your written permission to run a credit check. International students and newcomers often have no Canadian credit history. Most experienced London landlords know this and will work with you — be upfront about it, explain your situation, and offer a co-signer if possible.
5. **References** — a previous landlord if you've rented before, or a personal reference (a professor, an employer, a family friend who can vouch for you).
6. **First and last month's rent** — due on signing, once approved.

**If you have no Canadian credit history:** This is completely normal for international students and newcomers. Tell the landlord upfront. Offer to show bank statements showing you have the funds, provide a letter from your school or sponsor confirming your funding, and offer a guarantor or co-signer in Canada or internationally. Many London landlords have rented to Western students in this exact situation for years.

A **co-signer** is someone who agrees to be responsible for the rent if you can't pay. They co-sign the lease with you. It can be a parent, a family member, or any adult with a verifiable income.

## Rental Scams to Watch For

Students are a common target for rental fraud. A few things to know:

**Never e-transfer money before seeing the unit in person.** A landlord who asks for a deposit, first month's rent, or any money before you have seen the property is almost certainly running a scam. Legitimate landlords show you the property first.

**If the price is obviously below market, it isn't real.** A furnished two-bedroom on Broughdale for $800/month total does not exist in 2026. If it seems too good to be true, it is.

**Meet the actual landlord and walk through the real unit** — not just photos. Photos can be taken from a different building entirely. See it before you commit anything.

**Get everything in writing before any money changes hands.** Once you're approved, you should receive an Ontario Standard Lease to sign. This is a standard government form. Do not accept a handmade document that doesn't follow the standard lease format. The lease must include both signatures, the rent amount, what's included, and the start date.

**Ask who actually owns the property.** The City of London requires rental units to be licenced. You can ask the landlord for their rental licence number. Legitimate landlords will have no issue providing this.

## Understanding Your Lease at Western

Almost all Western-area rentals use the **Ontario Standard Lease** — a government-mandated form that protects both you and your landlord. A few things to look at carefully before you sign:

**The term.** Most student leases near Western are fixed for 12 months, usually May 1 to April 30, or September 1 to August 31. Read the dates. If you're on a co-op term or only here for eight months, a 12-month lease means you're responsible for rent for all 12 — even if you leave early. Talk to your landlord about assignment (transferring your lease to someone else) if you think you might need to leave before the end.

**What's included.** Utilities, parking, laundry, and internet need to be spelled out in the lease or a separate addendum. If it's not in writing, don't assume it's included.

**Last month's rent deposit.** Legal. Required. Held until the end of your tenancy and applied to your final month's rent.

**Additional terms.** Landlords can add their own terms to the lease — quiet hours, guest rules, parking rules. Read them. Some are reasonable house rules. Others may conflict with your rights under Ontario law — in which case those specific terms are not enforceable, even if you signed. When in doubt, contact Student Legal Services at Western, which offers free advice to Western students on tenant issues.

## Your Rights as a Tenant in Ontario

Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act protects you from the day you sign a lease. A few things every Western student should know:

**Rent can only go up once a year.** Once you've moved in, your landlord can only raise your rent after 12 months, and only by the provincially set guideline — 2.5% in 2026. They must give you 90 days written notice before any increase.

**Your landlord needs 24 hours notice to enter.** Except in emergencies, your landlord must give you written notice at least 24 hours in advance before entering your unit, and only between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m.

**Repairs are your landlord's job.** If something breaks, your landlord is legally required to fix it. Submit maintenance requests in writing (email is fine) and keep a record.

**A no-pets clause is generally unenforceable.** Under the RTA, a clause in your lease saying no pets is not always legally binding. If you have a pet, be honest about it upfront — but don't assume you'll be evicted for it.

**You cannot be evicted without due process.** If there's ever a dispute, your landlord cannot simply change the locks or kick you out. Any eviction requires a formal notice process and, in most cases, a hearing at the Landlord and Tenant Board. You have time and rights in that process.

For free legal advice on tenant issues, contact **Student Legal Services at Western** (they serve Western students specifically) or **Community Legal Education Ontario (CLEO)**.

## Tips for International Students Coming to London for Western

If you're arriving from outside Canada, a few things specific to your situation:

**You don't need a Social Insurance Number to rent.** Your passport and study permit are sufficient identification.

**No Canadian credit history is normal.** Be upfront about it and come prepared: bring your Western acceptance letter, financial documentation, and a co-signer if you have one. A guarantor letter from a Canadian contact also helps.

**Get tenant insurance.** It's not required by law, but it protects your belongings — electronics, clothing, furniture — in the event of fire, theft, or flood. Student policies typically cost $15–$25/month. You can buy them online in minutes. Some landlords now require it.

**Arrive with a plan for your first few nights.** If you're arriving in late August, units get handed over around September 1. Book a short-term stay (hotel, Airbnb, or Western's welcome housing program) so you're not scrambling for accommodations the moment you land.

**Western's International Student Identity Services can help.** They can connect you with housing resources, orientation programs, and other students who've been through the same process.

**January intake is much calmer.** If your program starts in January, you'll find more rental options and less competition. Many students who leave after their first semester open up units.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should I start looking for housing near Western?

For September move-in, start in January or February if you want the best options. Houses on the streets near campus go quickly. By April you're still in good shape for apartments. By July and August, you're competing for what's left — still possible, but you need to move fast when you find something.

### Can I rent near Western if I'm an international student with no Canadian credit?

Yes. London landlords who rent near Western are accustomed to working with international students. Be upfront about your situation, bring your enrollment letter and financial documentation, and offer a co-signer if you can. Many landlords will work with you.

### How much should I budget for rent near Western University in 2026?

A room in a shared student house runs $800–$1,100/month. A one-bedroom apartment near campus runs $1,450–$1,900/month. For a full house split among 4 students, expect $750–$1,050 per person. Budget separately for first and last month's rent upfront, plus utilities if they're not included.

### What is the Ontario Standard Lease and do I have to sign one?

The Ontario Standard Lease is a government-mandated lease form that must be used for most residential rentals in Ontario. It protects both tenants and landlords. Your landlord is required to provide it. If they offer you a handmade document instead, request the standard lease — it's your legal right.

### What happens if I need to leave my unit before my lease ends?

You are responsible for rent until the lease ends, unless you and your landlord agree in writing to end it early (using an N11 form), or you find someone to take over your lease through an assignment. Your landlord cannot unreasonably refuse an assignment. Western students on co-op terms often use this route — talk to your landlord early if you know your plans may change.

### Is it safe to rent a basement apartment near Western?

Basement apartments near Western are common and many are well-maintained. Before you commit, check: ceiling height (minimum 6 feet 11 inches), proper egress windows in bedrooms, working smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, and a City of London rental unit licence. Ask the landlord to show you the licence, or look it up on the City of London's website. Don't rent a basement that doesn't meet these standards.
